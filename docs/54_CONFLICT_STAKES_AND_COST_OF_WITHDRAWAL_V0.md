# 54 — Conflict Stakes and the Cost of Withdrawal, v0

**Status:** qualitative conflict-architecture pass using owner-supplied design direction. It answers the second gap the paper transition test exposed ([52](52_CULTIVATION_EMBODIMENT_TRANSITION_TEST_PAPER_PROTOTYPE_V0.md) §17) and resolves the qualitative core of AMO-Q028. It defines no mission, quest, reward, currency, loot, reputation, territory, ranking, matchmaking, criminal or legal system, NPC behaviour, numerical penalty, interface or code.

> **Withdrawal preserves the body by conceding something outside the body.**

## 1. Purpose

[36](36_COMBAT_RESOLUTION_SURRENDER_ESCAPE_AND_WITHDRAWAL_V0.md) settled *how* an encounter ends — surrender, escape, conscious withdrawal, objective resolution, and destruction only as escalation (AMO-D124–AMO-D128). It did not say **what is conceded**. Without that, Conscious Withdrawal is free preservation, and the central Amorpho decision — *I could still continue, but this objective is no longer worth risking this living manifestation* — cannot mean anything. This pass answers:

> What can a conflict place at stake, and what does the Warden concede by ending participation before biological destruction?

## 2. Stakes exist before combat

A fight does not create its own value. The stake comes from **why the actors are in conflict** — what one wants, what the other wants to prevent, and what becomes possible if one side stops resisting:

```
CONFLICT  →  a stake exists  →  combat is one way of contesting it
```

**A stake is not a reward.** Many conflicts are about preventing a loss: a Warden defending a rooted individual who withdraws may simply let the opponent into the area. No negative token is issued; the World continues from the new situation.

## 3. Stake families

Design families, not mission types. A conflict may combine several.

| Family | What is contested |
|---|---|
| **Access** | who can enter, pass through, remain in or use a place |
| **Position and presence** | who holds the immediate space after the encounter |
| **Protection** | whether something or someone stays shielded from the opponent's objective |
| **Custody** | who physically reaches, recovers, carries or keeps an object, plant or resource — custody only, never ownership (AMO-D065) |
| **Objective completion** | whether one side succeeds at a concrete task the other contests |
| **Time** | whether one side delays the other long enough for something elsewhere |
| **Information** *(future)* | whether discovery, observation or access to something is prevented |
| **Negotiated outcome** *(future)* | whether one side accepts a demand or yields a claim in the immediate dispute; no reputation or diplomacy |

**Access** supports clean non-destructive outcomes — *can the opponent pass?* — and **time** is first-class precisely because it needs no destruction: delaying long enough while another individual is moved, the Human reaches safety, a transition completes or someone else escapes.

## 4. The cost of withdrawal

> **The cost of leaving a fight is what stops being defended** (AMO-D172).

There is no arbitrary withdrawal penalty and no separate resource: no withdrawal tokens, courage penalty, surrender debuff or honour meter. **The consequence of withdrawal is that the unresolved conflict continues according to what was actually at stake.** Withdrawing from guarding a route lets the opponent pass; from contesting custody, lets them reach the object; from delaying, gives them time. Consequences are **causally grounded** in the actual conflict and **legible** enough that the Warden can understand *what happens if I stop resisting?* — uncertain perhaps, never random (AMO-D165).

**Withdrawal is not total defeat.** It concedes the immediate stake, wholly or partly, and leaves agency intact: the Warden may return, use another Amorpho, approach differently, involve others, or contest the larger conflict elsewhere (AMO-D128). The Warden can say *not with this body, not here, not now* without the game reading it as *you have lost everything*.

**Repetition has no global score.** A Warden who abandons objectives often simply has a different World history; there is no cowardice measure, and any future social reaction belongs to future systems (AMO-Q028).

## 5. The four families, read through the stake

The same family has different consequences in different conflicts. **There is no universal surrender penalty.**

| Family | Relationship to the stake |
|---|---|
| **Conscious Withdrawal** | the Warden disengages because continuing is not worth the risk; the stake is conceded as far as the context implies, and the conflict continues |
| **Surrender / Yield** | the Warden **explicitly accepts the opponent's immediate claim**; this may settle the local dispute more firmly than withdrawal |
| **Escape** | the Warden leaves **without accepting** the claim, preserving the manifestation and mobility while still abandoning the place, objective, protected thing or opportunity they leave behind; escape is contestable and is not free disengagement (AMO-D125) |
| **Objective Resolution** | the purpose of fighting ends — achieved, or made impossible — so neither side need concede and no body need fail |

When outnumbered, escape may save the manifestation while conceding the immediate stake — a strategic victory and a conflict defeat at once ([55](55_MULTI_ATTACKER_ENCOUNTERS_ESCAPE_AND_ANTI_DOGPILE_PRINCIPLES_V0.md) §10).

Objective Resolution shows why resolution must be objective-aware: an actor who only needed to delay the opponent wins when enough time passes, and continuing would be pointless.

**Surrender should normally stop ordinary hostile continuation.** Once the stake is conceded, continuing to attack a surrendered manifestation is ordinarily unnecessary. Enforcement and actors who ignore a yield are AMO-Q008 and AMO-Q028. Some future threats — a wild organism, a hostile non-Warden actor, an extraordinary situation — may not recognise surrender at all; ordinary Warden conflict is not generalised from them.

## 6. Biological risk against conflict value

At the risk decision the Warden compares two real losses:

| | Risks | Gains |
|---|---|---|
| **Continue** | protection loss, body damage, a lower Recovery Ceiling, Collapse, destruction, core exposure | keeping or achieving the stake |
| **Withdraw** | some or all of the immediate stake | the living manifestation and its future |

**Neither side is free.**

**The two values are incomparable, and stay so** (AMO-D173). No conversion exists — not *objective = 50, Leaf = 70* — and no system recommends an action. The game may surface biological information, the conflict's context and the visible state of the opponent; **the Warden judges** (AMO-D164, L71).

**Ordinary stakes sit between two failures:**

- if every stake is trivial, a rational Warden leaves at the first body risk and combat is hollow — so stakes must plausibly make a player think *one more exchange may actually be worth it*;
- if losing the immediate stake usually means catastrophic, permanent or campaign-ending loss, players fight to destruction every time and the resolution model collapses — so **ordinary stakes should be meaningful without making preservation irrational**.

No balance formula is defined.

**Both sides move during an encounter.** The stake can grow or shrink — the objective partly achieved, the protected target already gone, others arriving, the place no longer mattering, a new threat — and biological risk rises as protection degrades and damage lands. *The rational withdrawal point can change because the stake changes, not only because the body does.*

**The target moment:**

> The Warden believes the fight is still winnable, but decides the remaining objective is no longer worth what another serious hit could cost this particular individual.

**And continuing is equally legitimate.** *This objective is worth risking this manifestation* is a valid choice, with no morality system and no message calling it reckless (AMO-D127).

**Asymmetry is intended.** One side may care far more about the stake; one Warden may fight with a long-attuned, highly valued individual and the other with a biologically cheap body; their willingness to continue will differ, and nothing normalises it. A deeply attuned individual may simply be harder for the player to risk, with no mechanical penalty (AMO-D167, AMO-D169). **Different players may reasonably choose differently** and both be right by their own reasons.

**Scale varies.** Training, sport or controlled competition may carry deliberately low stakes and limited biological exposure; no mode is designed. At the other extreme, exceptional conflicts may genuinely justify risking destruction, core exposure and the individual itself, and should feel different because the stake is extraordinary — no special mode is needed for that yet.

## 7. Persistent-world consequence

> **Conflict consequences live in the World, not on a results screen** (AMO-D172).

The opponent remains at the location; reaches something; the Warden abandons access; a protected actor has to move; possession stays with the opponent; the objective cannot be completed now. **There is no arena reset**: whoever holds a place afterwards affects future rooting, access, rescue and travel. No persistence implementation is defined.

Particular cases follow from existing rules:

- **Other living Amorphos can be the stake** — protecting another rooted individual, reaching an exposed Tuber, preventing another actor from taking custody, rescuing a damaged individual. No rescue mission is designed.
- **An exposed Tuber is an especially strong physical stake**: after destruction the core lies where the body fell, and a later encounter may be about who reaches it (AMO-D130). Withdrawing may hand the other actor physical custody, which still grants **no ownership, binding, Attunement or authorization** (AMO-D065, AMO-D169).
- **Rooting sites and infrastructure can be contested** — a greenhouse, a favourable outdoor site, a protected area, a route — without becoming hit points or territory. No destructibility or territory system is defined.

## 8. Cultivation and combat feed each other

Cultivation creates valuable individuals, valuable sites, infrastructure and biological opportunities ([53](53_ROOTED_CULTIVATION_DECISION_SPACE_V0.md)); those can become things worth protecting. Combat then changes access, location, custody and safety, which changes later cultivation. **Each mode generates stakes for the other.**

That means a stake needs no quest giver. A Warden who has a valuable rooted plant, a greenhouse, an exposed Tuber, a route or a biological window already has reasons for conflict.

> **The stronger the persistent World becomes, the less combat needs artificial reasons to matter.**

Cultivation is not thereby militarised: most of what a Warden builds is never contested, and its value never depends on combat (AMO-D170).

## 9. The conflict decision frame

```
WHAT IS AT STAKE?
  ▼
WHAT HAPPENS IF I STOP RESISTING?
  ▼
WHAT BODY AM I RISKING — AND WHAT HAS ALREADY HAPPENED TO IT?
  ▼
HOW LIKELY DO I THINK CONTINUED SUCCESS IS?
  ▼
WARDEN JUDGEMENT ── continue · withdraw · surrender · escape
```

No calculation sits anywhere in it.

## 10. Paper-prototype integration

Doc 52's facilitator-invented stake is replaced by one minimal persistent-world **access** stake that reuses Card 1's sheltered place: an intruder is trying to get into the sheltered growing place the Warden built. If no Amorpho intervenes, it gets in and holds it, and that place is not usable for the Warden or X until the matter is taken up again — later, by some other means, or not at all. Withdrawing at Card 7 preserves X and lets the intruder in; continuing may still keep it out and risks X. The consequence then reaches cultivation: after a withdrawal, Card 9's rooting choice no longer includes the sheltered place.

Two failure criteria and two success criteria are added there: **withdrawal is free** (H) and **withdrawal is impossible to justify** (I), with the target between them; **genuine value conflict** (G) and **reasonable disagreement between players** (H). The diagnostic question is *was what you were protecting valuable enough that you genuinely considered risking another hit to X?* The test has still not been run.

## 11. Worked traces

**A — access.** An opponent tries to reach a protected area; the Warden contests it; X takes body damage and can still fight. Withdrawing lets the opponent in. Continuing keeps X at risk. Both costs are real.

**B — custody.** An exposed Tuber lies at a site and two actors contest physical access; the Warden's current Amorpho is damaged. Withdrawing may let the opponent reach the Tuber first; continuing puts the manifestation at further risk. Custody still is not ownership.

**C — delay.** The Warden must hold the opponent back long enough; the objective completes elsewhere; continuing no longer matters, and the encounter ends by Objective Resolution. No surrender, no destruction.

**D — cheap body, important objective.** The current manifestation has little productive opportunity left, its core still matters, the objective is important. The Warden may rationally accept more manifestation risk. No recommendation.

**E — attuned body, moderate objective.** The Warden fights with a long-known individual over something that matters but can be recovered later. Protection fails and the body is damaged. The Warden withdraws earlier than another player might — the relationship shaped human judgement, with no mechanical penalty.

## 13. Deferred concrete objective systems

AMO-Q028 keeps everything concrete: objective systems and their kinds (environmental, social, territorial, rescue, trade, exploration, sport), per-context consequence rules, escape contests and pursuit, PvP and training contexts, social and legal outcomes, and any reward or economy integration. AMO-Q008 keeps surrender enforcement and hostile actors who ignore a yield. AMO-Q130 keeps Human involvement; AMO-Q091 and AMO-Q093 custody, binding and ownership consequences. No new question was required.

**Result:** a stake gives the Warden a reason to keep going; a living body gives them a reason to stop. The best Amorpho fights live between those two pressures — and a Warden may lose the encounter and still have made the right decision for the individual they will be fighting with next year.
