# 21 — Pathological Tuber Impact, v0

**Status:** specification, version 0. This document answers AMO-Q108 at the conceptual level: under what biological circumstances an adverse situation becomes harmful persistent Tuber loss rather than temporary impairment, stress, reserve use or lost growth. It defines no formulas, thresholds, percentages, durations or species facts.

It completes the harm model begun in [18_PHASE_DAMAGE_CORE_RECOVERY_AND_BLOOM_MATURITY_V0.md](18_PHASE_DAMAGE_CORE_RECOVERY_AND_BLOOM_MATURITY_V0.md) and depends on [19_DEVELOPMENTAL_MATURITY_V0.md](19_DEVELOPMENTAL_MATURITY_V0.md) and [20_BIOLOGY_MAGIC_BOUNDARY_AND_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md](20_BIOLOGY_MAGIC_BOUNDARY_AND_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md).

> Leaf produces. Bloom spends. Pathology takes.
> Visible damage is not the verdict. The persistent Tuber outcome is the verdict.

## 1. Definition

**Pathological Tuber Impact** is harmful biological consequence that reaches the persistent Tuber: actual loss of its integrity or accumulated development, with possible effects on stored capacity and future potential. No temporary manifestation needs to be present (AMO-D090, AMO-D092).

It is conceptually distinct from leaf integrity loss, bloom integrity loss, stress load, temporary reserve use, lost growth opportunity, and **Programmed Tuber Draw**.

### It is not Programmed Tuber Draw

| | **Programmed Tuber Draw** | **Pathological Tuber Impact** |
|---|---|---|
| What | normal biological expenditure for a legitimate process | harmful loss from adverse circumstances |
| Example | **Bloom** | the routes in §2 |
| The plant is | doing what its biology is meant to do | being harmed |
| Tuber resources | **spent** | **lost** |

Both reduce persistent resources; both recover while the individual lives (AMO-D075, AMO-D087). Nothing may classify Bloom expenditure as injury merely because Tuber resources decline — no injury alert, no pathological terminology, no damage status (§7).

The [replacement-routing specification](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md) adds another legitimate **manifestation investment**: funding a new Leaf after functional collapse. That expenditure carries a real future cost but is **not Pathological Tuber Impact by itself**. Whether adverse circumstances later cause actual persistent loss remains the separate outcome test in this document (AMO-D092, AMO-D096).

During [Emergence](30_EMERGENCE_INVESTMENT_AND_FULL_DEPLOYMENT_ACCOUNTING_BOUNDARY_V0.md), sunk construction investment can be lost if a developing Leaf or Bloom fails, especially after substantial commitment. This can leave the Tuber depleted without automatically injuring it or regressing Developmental Maturity (AMO-D109, AMO-D092). After Full Deployment, damage belongs first to the mature manifestation and reaches persistent Tuber state only through the applicable downstream biological pathway (AMO-D111). **Direct harmful exposure to the Tuber itself** remains the separate pathway below; the accounting handoff does not remove it.

## 2. Two pathways

Pathology reaches the Tuber by two routes, and the architecture supports both (AMO-D090).

### A. Direct Tuber Impact

The persistent Tuber itself encounters harmful environmental conditions or physical compromise. This can happen while rooted, dormant, excavated or in transit. Which exposures matter, and under what circumstances, remains AMO-Q117; no mechanism, threshold or tolerance is defined here.

```
World environment  +  Tuber biological response  +  duration
        →  direct persistent Tuber consequence
```

This may occur **whether or not a leaf is present**, and it bypasses the productivity pathway entirely.

### B. Indirect Tuber Impact

The active manifestation fails to generate enough biological productivity, and harmful circumstances eventually cause actual persistent Tuber loss. Insufficient growth by itself may end in no gain, without pathology:

```
productive capacity becomes inadequate
    → biological demands may go unmet
    → persistent development may stall without pathology
    → actual persistent loss may eventually occur
```

This is the route a damaged leaf or a chronically poor active-phase environment takes, and it is the one most easily got wrong (§3).

### Acute and cumulative

Both routes may be **acute** — a single severe event — or **cumulative**: persistent poor Fit, insufficient leaf productivity, repeated incomplete recovery, or prolonged unsuitable unrooted conditions.

> A slow grinding biological deficit may become serious without any single catastrophic moment.

A discrete event can be sufficient, but is never required.

## 3. Visible damage is not the verdict

**No percentage of visible structure lost may directly determine persistent loss** — a damaged-Leaf percentage cannot itself decide whether or how much Tuber loss occurred (AMO-D091, AMO-D079).

Visible structural damage is **one input** into biological productivity, and the persistent outcome depends on what biological work was actually achieved or lost.

### Leaf Functional Capacity

> How much useful biological work the current leaf manifestation can still perform.

Working terminology. It is **not** identical to leaf integrity: two leaves with similar structural damage may retain different functional capability, depending on what was damaged and how. No formula is defined (AMO-Q116).

### Remaining Productive Opportunity

> How much useful active-phase biological opportunity remains before the current manifestation naturally ends.

This is the factor most often missing from damage models, and it is decisive. **It is biological opportunity, not a UI countdown**, and must not be modelled as a visible timer (AMO-Q116).

### The productive outcome

```
Leaf Functional Capacity  +  Environmental Fit
    +  Remaining Productive Opportunity
    +  Current Condition  +  Reserves
              ▼
    productive biological outcome
              ▼
    persistent Tuber consequence
```

Not a formula. The point is that **the Tuber consequence follows the biological work, not the appearance** (L51).

## 4. Timing changes opportunity, not the verdict by itself

The same visible damage at different points in an active phase can produce opposite persistent outcomes. Timing changes what biological work remains possible; it does not assign a fixed result (AMO-D091):

| | **Earlier damage** | **Later damage** |
|---|---|---|
| Opportunity | more productive time may remain for recovery or compensation; prolonged lost function can also accumulate | less productive time remains to replace work already missed; earlier successful work may also buffer the loss |
| Possible persistent outcome | no loss, lost growth only, or actual loss | no loss, lost growth only, or actual loss |

> **Gameplay impairment and persistent biological loss are separate questions.**

A fighter can be wrecked for the rest of the season while the plant finishes the year in good shape — and that is not a bug, it is the model working.

### Environment after the damage matters

A damaged leaf in an excellent environment may still do meaningful biological work; the same leaf in poor conditions may become inadequate. **Leaf damage alone never determines the Tuber outcome** — what happens *next* participates (AMO-D091).

### Compensation is possible, not guaranteed

Modest early impairment may be compensated later if functional capacity stays sufficient, Fit is excellent and enough opportunity remains. This is **not** a catch-up bonus; it simply means early damage does not mathematically guarantee regression.

The reverse does **not** hold: good late conditions cannot recreate productive time already gone. Opportunity is directional (AMO-Q116).

## 5. The diagnostic

The cleanest rule, and the one that settles every ambiguous case (AMO-D092):

> **Pathological Tuber Impact has occurred when the persistent Tuber has actually lost biological integrity or accumulated development because of harmful circumstances.**

Possible persistent outputs: **vitality reduction**, **developmental maturity regression**, or both.

The other side of that line is now defined positively as **ordinary depletion** — reduced available capacity from legitimate expenditure, insufficient replenishment or missed return, while persistent structure, capability and condition remain fundamentally recoverable through ordinary future biology ([43](43_ORDINARY_DEPLETION_TO_PERSISTENT_HARM_BOUNDARY_V0.md), AMO-D148). Crossing into harm **changes the ontology** rather than the reading on a meter, and cumulative strain crosses only where persistence itself degrades (AMO-D149). Severity is not classification: a dangerously depleted Tuber may be entirely intact.

**Not qualifying, on their own:**

- reserve consumption — reserves are a buffer, and spending them is what they are for;
- stress load — however high, it recovers without persistent loss (AMO-D056);
- lost expected growth — *lost opportunity is not regression* (AMO-D082).

### Three productive-phase outcomes

Apart from normal Programmed Tuber Draw, a productive phase may leave the persistent organism **gaining**, **approximately maintaining**, or **losing to harmful circumstances**. The third case short of harm is **insufficient Active Leaf Productive Return** — little gain, no net gain or depletion relative to investment — and it is **not** pathology by itself ([39](39_ACTIVE_LEAF_PRODUCTIVE_RETURN_AND_PERSISTENT_TUBER_BENEFIT_V0.md), AMO-D138). Only actual persistent loss in the third case is Pathological Tuber Impact. A disappointing season may produce no gain without any regression at all (AMO-D082). These are qualitative outcomes, not an `inputs - outputs` calculation; Bloom's expected draw cannot be diagnosed from its net change.

### On "Tuber Balance" — considered and not retained

A named diagnostic concept, *Tuber Balance* (is the Tuber gaining, holding or losing over an interval), was evaluated and **deliberately not adopted**.

It fails on the case this document exists to protect: **a blooming individual's Tuber balance is negative by design.** Programmed Draw makes the balance fall without any pathology, so `balance negative → pathology` is wrong, and the term invites exactly that inference. Qualifying it — *balance net of Programmed Draw* — turns the model into the accounting math the concept was meant to avoid.

The useful distinction remains in the productive-phase outcomes above. They do not classify Bloom's intended spending as harm.

### Vitality and Maturity move independently

A pathological event may reduce vitality primarily, regress maturity primarily, or affect both — in no fixed proportion (AMO-D092). Conceptually, **failed productivity** tends to cost development, while **direct tissue harm** tends to compromise integrity. That tendency is not a mapping and must not be implemented as one (AMO-Q118).

### Severity is continuous

The **boundary** is binary — persistent loss did or did not occur — but once it has, depth varies enormously: tiny regression, meaningful setback, severe multi-cycle setback, near-total developmental collapse. **All recoverable while the individual survives** (AMO-D075).

## 6. Susceptibility and buffering

**Current condition modifies susceptibility.** The same adverse exposure may or may not cross into pathology depending on vitality, stress load, reserves, developmental maturity and life-cycle state. An already compromised individual crosses sooner. No function is defined (AMO-Q118).

**Reserves buffer, but are not a shield meter.** An adverse period may draw on reserves; continued demand may deplete them; an insufficient buffer can leave persistent development increasingly vulnerable. But **direct severe Tuber harm may bypass gradual depletion entirely** — §2's two pathways are genuinely different.

**Stress load is a warning, not the injury.** High stress may signal sustained pressure and often precedes regression, but `stress ≠ pathology` and stress can fully recover with nothing persistent lost (AMO-D056).

## 7. Interactions with the rest of the model

### Premature senescence can be protective

Retreat may **prevent** deeper pathology:

```
leaf productivity failing → continued active state increasingly costly
    → senescence begins → manifestation abandoned → Tuber protected
```

Outcomes may be lost opportunity only, a reserve cost, mild regression, or a severe vitality compromise **avoided**. So premature senescence does **not** automatically mean Pathological Tuber Impact occurred — it may be the reason it did not (AMO-D092, AMO-Q087).

### Replenishment is not repair

**Replenishment** restores available capacity through ordinary successful biology; **persistent recovery or repair** addresses actual harm. They are separate processes, an individual may need both at once, and no single notion of health may absorb them ([43](43_ORDINARY_DEPLETION_TO_PERSISTENT_HARM_BOUNDARY_V0.md), AMO-D150). Repair, treatment and reversibility remain open (AMO-Q102, AMO-Q105), and persistent pathology stays a deliberately deferred future layer: this architecture needs the boundary, not a disease system.

### Dormancy is not automatic Tuber recovery

Dormancy stops some active biological demands; it does **not** automatically restore vitality or maturity, which follow biological processes (AMO-D076, AMO-D083).

Deep Dormancy **does** fully restore **Astral Readiness** — but that is magical state, and confusing the two would suggest a compromised plant emerges healed (AMO-D086). It does not.

### Programmed Draw and pathology can coexist

A blooming individual may experience normal Programmed Tuber Draw **and**, separately, harmful environmental pressure causing pathology. The two are conceptually additive but not the same process, and must stay separable so a later system can explain **what actually happened** to a plant rather than only that it got smaller.

### Bloom spending is expected

The player should understand that Bloom costs stored development and that a later leaf phase rebuilds it. No pathological framing may attach to it (AMO-D087).

### Leaf rebuilds — including after pathology

A productive leaf period is the primary current mechanism for reserve replenishment, Tuber development, maturity increase, rebuilding after Bloom Draw **and rebuilding after pathology** (AMO-D081). It is not assumed to be the only future mechanism.

### Magical embodiment suspends all of this

While astrally inhabited, normal biological simulation is suspended, so **Pathological Tuber Impact does not accumulate from combat, movement, time embodied or magical ability use** (AMO-D084, L49). This question concerns biological existence. When the individual returns to rooted life, biological consequences resume.

**Astral Readiness is not a Tuber health proxy.** An individual may be astrally fully ready and developmentally regressed, or biologically excellent and astrally exhausted (AMO-D086).

### Recovery, and death

All pathological loss is recoverable while the individual lives: vitality restored, maturity rebuilt, Bloom eligibility regained, former scale eventually exceeded — at the cost of biological time and successful growth (AMO-D075, AMO-D083).

Pathology may become severe enough that **viability is lost**, and death is terminal (AMO-Q104). But **extreme regression is not death**: a tiny surviving Tuber may still represent living biological continuity, with identity unresolved (AMO-Q094).

## 8. Excavated and unrooted Tubers

The Human layer physically excavates and transports Tubers. **Being unrooted is not automatically harmful** — but it is not stasis either (AMO-D093):

> A biological Tuber does not become timeless because a Human is carrying it.

An excavated Tuber remains a biological individual whose local environment continues to matter. Prolonged unsuitable conditions may eventually cause actual persistent loss; mere excavation need not. No specific harm mechanism, storage rule, timing or tolerance is defined (AMO-Q117).

An individual that is **ready to emerge or actively transitioning** may be harmed if excavated and not placed into suitable substrate while its biological demands continue unmet. Timing is not encoded (AMO-Q117).

This is deliberately not an inventory abstraction. It creates real Human responsibilities — excavate at appropriate times, provide appropriate transport conditions, replant, choose suitable locations, avoid prolonged unsuitable exposure — without any logistics interface or transport equipment being designed (L23, AMO-D062).

## 9. No single numerical threshold

The **Core-Impact Threshold** remains useful as conceptual shorthand for *the boundary where consequences become persistent* — but it was never a number and must not become one (AMO-D079, AMO-D091).

Crossing may result from direct severe exposure, cumulative deficit, repeated insufficient recovery, developmental context or timing. There is no universal value, and any documentation implying otherwise is wrong.

## 10. Worked cases

Non-numeric, species-neutral, no real biology.

| Case | What happens |
|---|---|
| **A — severe-looking early damage, compensation succeeds** | substantial function remains; much productive opportunity remains; Fit improves; reserves are adequate; later productivity compensates. **No persistent Tuber loss.** |
| **B — modest-looking late damage, phase fails** | function is strongly compromised; little productive opportunity remains; Fit stays poor; reserves are depleted; recovery does not catch up; persistent development regresses. **Indirect Pathological Tuber Impact.** |
| **C — similar damage, different timing** | with the same structural loss, either early recovery may compensate or earlier successful work may make a late loss tolerable; with poor function and insufficient opportunity at either point, loss remains possible. **Timing alone does not decide.** |
| **D — prolonged poor environment, leaf intact** | leaf visually present throughout; Fit stays poor; output stays insufficient; development regresses over time. **Indirect pathology with no dramatic visible damage.** |
| **E — dormant excavated Tuber, unsuitable conditions** | no active Leaf exists; harmful exposure persists until actual Tuber integrity is lost. **Direct and cumulative pathology without manifestation damage.** |
| **F — Bloom, then a good leaf cycle** | Bloom creates Programmed Draw — **no pathology**; a later productive leaf phase rebuilds what was spent and may exceed the prior developmental state. **Spending is not harm.** |
| **G — Bloom under harmful conditions** | normal Programmed Draw proceeds **and** poor conditions separately cause harmful compromise. Both occur; they are not merged, so the outcome remains explicable. |

Cases A and B show the decisive reversal: severe appearance can end with no persistent loss, while modest appearance can end with actual loss. Case C shows why timing informs opportunity without imposing a direction.

## 11. What this settles, and what it does not

**Settled conceptually:** what Pathological Tuber Impact means · Direct versus Indirect pathways · acute versus cumulative · the role of timing · Leaf Functional Capacity · Remaining Productive Opportunity · the outcome-based diagnostic · the distinction from Programmed Tuber Draw · excavated Tubers remaining simulated · premature senescence as potentially protective.

**Not settled, deliberately:** formulas · the productivity model's internals · susceptibility functions · exact reserve and stress dynamics · storage and transport tolerances · direct environmental susceptibility · species parameters · where neutral becomes negative.

> Model completion is not numerical completion.
