# 30 — Emergence Investment and Full-Deployment Accounting Boundary, v0

**Status:** conceptual accounting/ownership boundary using owner-supplied domain direction. It applies to primary Leaf, Replacement Leaf and Bloom within the shared [Manifestation Emergence](29_REPLACEMENT_EMERGENCE_TO_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md) topology. It defines no resource currency, cost curve, damage formula, stage duration, species fact, combat mapping or implementation.

> **The Tuber builds the manifestation. The mature manifestation then performs for the Tuber.**

## 1. Tuber-funded construction

The Tuber is the persistent biological individual and investor (AMO-D095). Commitment starts biological construction; it does not pay a complete fixed fee or yield a mature body at once. Through Emergence Sheath formation, surface emergence and deployment, the developing Leaf or Bloom remains unfinished **Tuber-funded construction**. Biological capacity, time and remaining opportunity are being spent, while the mature manifestation's expected role has not yet been delivered. For v0, the value of this incomplete construction stays conceptually on the Tuber side. This is ownership language, not a single numeric balance or stored ledger.

The same rule applies regardless of whether the Tuber chose Replacement autonomously or the Warden compelled a feasible route. Warden authorship does not change funding, exposure, timing or failure cost (AMO-D100, L52). Strong Tuber capacity and favorable Environmental Fit do not make construction instantaneous or refund what was spent. Human protection may change real World conditions; it does not reverse sunk expenditure by command (AMO-D035–AMO-D037, AMO-D062).

## 2. Developmental commitment and sunk investment

**Emergence cost accumulates with developmental commitment.** As the Tuber builds more of the manifestation, generally more biological capacity has been spent and more is at risk if it fails. This is a qualitative direction, not a linear curve or a claim that external size precisely measures cost. Developmental commitment is a conceptual property of the construction history, not a new score, Developmental Maturity, visible height or cosmetic completeness (AMO-D080, AMO-Q084, AMO-Q101).

For a comparable attempt, **failure late in Emergence costs more already-invested Tuber capacity than failure early**, because more construction has occurred without the mature manifestation's return. A failure shortly before Full Deployment can leave the greatest sunk-construction exposure. Less productive opportunity may also remain, making another attempt less useful or infeasible (AMO-D091, AMO-Q116). These are distinct reasons for a poor outcome; neither is an automatic injury verdict.

Once spent, investment is not reset by failed emergence, astral exit/re-entry, Warden selection or later favorable conditions. Subsequent healthy biology may rebuild capacity over time; it does not rewrite the earlier cost. A further attempt requires a new feasible Tuber commitment, further biological time and additional expenditure. No automatic or unlimited retry follows (AMO-D096, AMO-D108, AMO-Q101).

### Damage severity and investment at risk are separate

| Question | What it describes |
|---|---|
| **Developing-structure damage severity** | How much the incomplete manifestation is impaired, and whether it can continue; exact response remains open (AMO-Q086). |
| **Tuber investment at risk** | How much construction the Tuber has already funded at this developmental point; exact accounting remains open (AMO-Q101). |
| **Remaining Productive Opportunity** | What useful future activity or another attempt could still achieve; routing remains open (AMO-Q116). |

Severe-looking early damage may terminate an attempt with relatively little sunk investment. Modest-looking damage near Full Deployment may prevent completion after substantial investment. Appearance can inform assessment, but cannot directly determine the lost Tuber investment or Pathological Tuber Impact (AMO-D091, L51). Damage can also permit continued construction and [imperfect Full Deployment](31_DAMAGED_EMERGENCE_TO_IMPERFECT_DEPLOYMENT_V0.md); exact response remains AMO-Q086.

## 3. Full Deployment: the hard accounting handoff

**Full Deployment ends Tuber-funded construction and begins mature-manifestation performance and damage ownership** (AMO-D111). It means the current Leaf or Bloom has completed its primary biological emergence architecture, not merely that it looks large or visible. Exact biological completion criteria remain AMO-Q084. The boundary is the same for a smaller Replacement Leaf: fully deployed means complete **for that manifestation**, not equal to the primary Leaf in scale.

| Before Full Deployment | At and after Full Deployment |
|---|---|
| Incomplete Leaf or Bloom is Tuber-funded construction. | Mature Leaf or Bloom owns its manifestation-specific structural, functional and damage state. |
| Failure loses already-invested construction and may leave no useful mature manifestation. | Damage is assessed first in the mature manifestation, with recovery and performance handled there. |
| Developing-stage damage and continuation remain AMO-Q086. | Mature Leaf uses [23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md) and [24](24_SAME_PHASE_LEAF_RECOVERY_V0.md); mature Bloom keeps its own damage domain. |
| Normal inhabitation is unavailable. | Normal inhabitation may become possible only through the separate access and Astral Readiness gates (AMO-D107). |

This is a **hard conceptual handoff**, with no fractional pre-deployment Leaf/Bloom ownership and no new data field. It changes accounting and damage domain, not organism identity: the Tuber remains the persistent individual, and its costs, condition, Developmental Maturity and history continue. A damaged attempt may continue to Full Deployment; the mature state receives what was actually built, without repair at the boundary ([31](31_DAMAGED_EMERGENCE_TO_IMPERFECT_DEPLOYMENT_V0.md)). Full Deployment is not Astral Readiness, perfect health, maximum scale, or a guarantee of future productivity.

After deployment a Leaf's mature biological work may support future Tuber condition and development (AMO-D081, AMO-Q116). A Bloom begins its mature reproductive role while its **Programmed Tuber Draw may continue**; the handoff does not cancel that normal expenditure or turn it into pathology (AMO-D087). Mature performance and its consequences feed back toward the persistent Tuber on their own phase-specific terms.

## 4. Mature damage and persistent consequence

After Full Leaf Deployment, injury first changes that Leaf's Structural Integrity, Leaf Functional Capacity, recovery prospect and subsequent work. It does **not** immediately debit equivalent Tuber integrity, reserves or Developmental Maturity. Any later persistent consequence follows the existing function, opportunity, condition and [Pathological Tuber Impact](21_PATHOLOGICAL_TUBER_IMPACT_V0.md) pathways (AMO-D079, AMO-D090–AMO-D092). A mature Leaf can scar and stabilize without persistent Tuber loss; poor sustained performance can later contribute to it.

After Full Bloom Deployment, injury first belongs to the Bloom manifestation. Its effects on mature Bloom function, reproductive outcome, continued resource draw or lifecycle may eventually affect the persistent Tuber; the details remain open (AMO-Q086, AMO-Q101). Bloom damage is not automatically Tuber injury or maturity regression. Separately, **direct harmful exposure to the Tuber itself** remains an existing possible route to Pathological Tuber Impact, whether or not any manifestation exists (AMO-D090). That is not a shortcut from mature manifestation damage.

Failed construction can leave a Tuber depleted, alive and structurally intact. Reserve use, lost opportunity and a year with no gain are not automatically Pathological Tuber Impact or Developmental Maturity regression (AMO-D082, AMO-D092). Repeated failures may worsen future conditions enough for actual harmful persistent loss; that outcome is evaluated downstream, never inferred from sunk spending alone (AMO-Q118). No later successful manifestation refunds an earlier failed attempt.

```text
PERSISTENT TUBER
  → funds Emergence as developmental commitment accumulates
  → Full Deployment: construction accounting hands off
  → mature Leaf or Bloom performs, suffers damage, and follows its phase-specific recovery rules
  → phase-specific outcome may affect persistent Tuber state downstream
```

## 5. Worked boundary traces

**A — early Emergence loss.** The Tuber commits and starts the Emergence Sheath/path. Severe damage destroys the developing structure early. No mature manifestation exists. Some investment has been spent, but much potential construction had not yet been committed; the viable Tuber reevaluates future routing. The visible severity does not itself identify the sunk cost.

**B — late Emergence loss.** A developing manifestation nears Full Deployment. Considerable Tuber construction is already funded when an adverse event prevents completion. No mature manifestation has assumed performance ownership; little or no mature return has been obtained. The Tuber retains the sunk cost and now has less remaining opportunity for another attempt. This can be a severe biological year without automatic pathology or maturity regression.

**C — similar damage, different commitment.** Similar visible disruption can occur early or late. The effect on continuation may differ, and the late case exposes more already-invested Tuber capacity. Damage severity, sunk investment and opportunity require separate assessments; none is a proxy for the others.

**D — mature Leaf damage.** A Leaf completes Full Deployment. A later event damages its structure. The Leaf owns the immediate Structural Integrity and Functional Capacity change, and its mature recovery model evaluates the next trajectory. The Tuber receives no automatic equivalent damage debit; only downstream biological consequences can change its persistent state.

**E — Bloom and Replacement.** Before Full Bloom Deployment, failure loses Tuber-funded Bloom construction. After deployment, Bloom damage belongs first to Bloom state while normal Programmed Draw remains distinct. A reduced Replacement Leaf crosses the **same Full Deployment handoff** as a primary Leaf; Warden authorship does not change its sunk-cost risk or mature damage ownership.

## 6. Deferred mechanics and acceptance

AMO-Q084 owns exact Full Deployment criteria, emergence sub-phases and repeated-attempt topology. AMO-Q101 owns investment distribution, biological variables, failure/continuation routing and effects on later eligibility. AMO-Q086 owns damage response and the mechanisms of imperfect continuation during Emergence, traced conceptually in [31](31_DAMAGED_EMERGENCE_TO_IMPERFECT_DEPLOYMENT_V0.md), and mature Bloom damage. AMO-Q116 owns mature productivity and Remaining Productive Opportunity; AMO-Q118 owns the later crossing into actual persistent loss. No new question, resource field or physiology formula is introduced.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Early versus late failure | Later commitment generally leaves more sunk Tuber construction, with no mature manifestation return in either failure. |
| Damage severity versus cost | Comparable visible damage can carry different sunk cost at different developmental points. |
| Full Deployment | Construction accounting ends; mature performance and damage ownership begin; Astral Readiness stays separate. |
| Leaf, Replacement, Bloom | All share the handoff; mature phase roles and Bloom Programmed Draw remain distinct. |
| Pathology and maturity | Failed expenditure alone establishes neither Pathological Tuber Impact nor Developmental Maturity regression. |
| History and retries | Earlier investment remains spent; another attempt needs new capacity and time. |

**Result:** while a manifestation is Emerging, its biological value stays on the Tuber side. Full Deployment hands mature performance and immediate damage ownership to the Leaf or Bloom. Persistent Tuber consequences after that boundary require their own biological path.
