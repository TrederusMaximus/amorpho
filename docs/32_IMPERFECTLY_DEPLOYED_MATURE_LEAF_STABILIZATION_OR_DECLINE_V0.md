# 32 — Imperfectly Deployed Mature Leaf: Stabilization or Decline, v0

**Status:** conceptual mature-state trace using owner-supplied domain direction. It begins where [31](31_DAMAGED_EMERGENCE_TO_IMPERFECT_DEPLOYMENT_V0.md) ends — a Leaf that crossed **Full Deployment** already compromised — and follows its first mature period to an updated mature Leaf state. It defines no species response, recovery rate, extent, threshold, duration, formula, numeric capacity, productivity integration, persistent Tuber outcome, routing decision or combat mapping.

> **Full Deployment completes construction; it does not perfect it. A mature Leaf may begin life imperfect, stabilize, and still do useful work.**

## 1. Purpose

[30](30_EMERGENCE_INVESTMENT_AND_FULL_DEPLOYMENT_ACCOUNTING_BOUNDARY_V0.md) set the accounting boundary at Full Deployment. [31](31_DAMAGED_EMERGENCE_TO_IMPERFECT_DEPLOYMENT_V0.md) established that a damaged Emergence can still reach that boundary and that the mature manifestation receives what biology actually built (AMO-D112, AMO-D113). This trace answers the next question: **what happens during the first mature period of a Leaf that deployed already compromised.** It ends with a mature Leaf functional trajectory and stops there.

## 2. Starting handed-off state

No new damage event is required to begin. The Leaf begins mature life already imperfect. It may be scarred, asymmetric, structurally reduced, functionally reduced, mechanically compromised or otherwise developmentally imperfect, as the actual construction outcome dictates. There is no normalization step, no minimum “healthy mature” baseline, no repair at the boundary and no refund (AMO-D113).

| Carried across Full Deployment | Not supplied by Full Deployment |
|---|---|
| The actual **Structural Integrity** biology built. | Pristine or symmetrical structure. |
| The actual **Leaf Functional Capacity** biology built. | A restored or averaged starting capacity. |
| Developmental defects, present as current state. | Erasure of those defects. |
| Mature damage ownership, now held by the Leaf (AMO-D111). | A further Tuber debit for having deployed imperfectly. |
| The persistent Tuber, its condition, maturity and history (AMO-D058, AMO-D095). | Astral Readiness or any access gate (AMO-D107). |

The Tuber's construction expenditure remains spent. **Imperfect Full Deployment is not failed Emergence:** a mature Leaf exists, and it is a valid mature manifestation.

## 3. Mature-domain ownership

From this boundary on, the mature Leaf model is authoritative. Later change uses [23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md) and [24](24_SAME_PHASE_LEAF_RECOVERY_V0.md) — Structural Integrity, Leaf Functional Capacity, stabilization, mature scarring, functional recovery, recovery prospect, ongoing deterioration and **Remaining Productive Opportunity**. Emergence accounting is not re-entered for anything that happens after deployment (AMO-D111, L55).

Domain separation is unchanged. The **World** owns subsequent local conditions; **Environmental Fit** derives the biological boundary and heals nothing by decree; the **Amorpho** owns current condition, the Leaf's structure and function, and their change over time (AMO-D035–AMO-D037). The trajectory belongs to that biological evolution, not to a new subsystem.

Developmental history is preserved as **history**, not as a re-chargeable account. The mature model does not need every event that caused a defect; it receives the resulting state.

```text
DEVELOPMENTAL DAMAGE
  → IMPERFECT FULL DEPLOYMENT
  → INITIAL MATURE LEAF STATE
  → Environmental Fit + current condition + time
  → UPDATED MATURE LEAF STATE (improving, stable or declining)
```

## 4. Inherited impairment and new mature damage

Two origins can describe one current state:

| Origin | What it describes | Where its rules live |
|---|---|---|
| **Inherited developmental impairment** | Limitation acquired **before** Full Deployment and embodied in what was built. | Development: AMO-Q086, AMO-Q101 ([31](31_DAMAGED_EMERGENCE_TO_IMPERFECT_DEPLOYMENT_V0.md)) |
| **New mature-state damage** | Injury occurring **after** Full Deployment to the built form. | Mature Leaf: [23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md), AMO-Q086 |

They coexist without merging. Current Structural Integrity and Functional Capacity may reflect both; their **historical origin** does not collapse into one damage description, and neither origin is a stored damage value. Similar appearances can have different histories (AMO-D091, L51).

## 5. Stabilization, functional recovery and structural limits

Mature recovery cannot rebuild missing architecture into the pristine manifestation that would have existed without developmental damage (AMO-D094). This matters most here, precisely because the Leaf begins mature life imperfect. What mature life can offer is **stabilization** of the present state, sealing or scarring, mechanical stabilization where possible, compensation by surviving structure, preservation of remaining viable structure, and improved useful function through it.

Stabilization and compensation may act on an **inherited** defect just as they may on later injury: a developmental defect can become mechanically stabilized, scarred over or functionally compensated during mature life. That changes the Leaf's present condition; it does not rewrite its construction history or restore the construction that never happened.

**Structural Integrity and Leaf Functional Capacity remain separate.** A Leaf may begin mature life with reduced structure and surprisingly useful function, or with apparently adequate structure and poor function. Neither is derived from the other, and neither is read from appearance (AMO-D091, L51). **Recovery prospect is assessed from the actual mature starting state**, not inferred from the developmental history that produced it: an imperfectly deployed Leaf may have substantial, moderate or very limited stabilization potential. Prospect is still not actual recovery ([24](24_SAME_PHASE_LEAF_RECOVERY_V0.md), AMO-Q103).

One conceptual distinction is worth recording without deciding it: developmental damage may leave this manifestation with a **lower attainable functional ceiling** than an undamaged equivalent would have had. Mature recovery would then improve function toward what this Leaf can attain, which is not a return to a hypothetical undamaged original. Whether such a ceiling exists, how it arises and how it is represented remain open (AMO-Q103, AMO-Q086). No cap, proportion, rate or value is defined.

## 6. Favorable, adequate and poor Environmental Fit

The same imperfect starting state can diverge under different conditions. These are conceptual trajectories, not a classification of environments or an exhaustive outcome list:

| Subsequent conditions | Possible mature trajectory |
|---|---|
| **Favorable Fit, adequate biological condition** | Stabilization, then improved Functional Capacity and useful continued mature performance. Structural defects may remain plainly visible. |
| **Adequate but not strongly favorable Fit** | Neither major recovery nor major decline: a **stable compromised state** that continues to do reduced but real work. |
| **Poor Fit** | Existing weakness worsens function, stability declines, and a future routing threshold may eventually become relevant. |

Not every imperfect Leaf must either heal strongly or deteriorate; **equilibrium is a legitimate outcome.** Divergence under identical starting biology reinforces the separation of domains: the World supplies conditions, and the Amorpho's own response produces the trajectory.

Current biological condition, including the persistent Tuber's state, may influence recovery capacity, stabilization and resilience ([14](14_CURRENT_BIOLOGICAL_CONDITION_V0.md), AMO-Q103). That is a biological-state dependency, not a support-flow model, and it does not return damage ownership to the Tuber (§8). No time step, duration or rate accompanies any of these trajectories.

## 7. The decline boundary

Sustained decline may eventually reduce mature function enough that the Leaf can no longer serve as the active manifestation. **This trace stops at that boundary**, which is specified in [33](33_MATURE_LEAF_FUNCTIONAL_COLLAPSE_BOUNDARY_V0.md): while recovery and stabilization still preserve a serviceable Leaf it remains viable; when deterioration irreversibly outruns them, **Collapse Commitment** is reached, the Leaf enters a terminal period and the manifestation eventually ends (AMO-D114, AMO-D115). Only then does routing evaluate biological eligibility and Environmental Fit, from the persistent Tuber ([25](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md), [26](26_PLANT_AUTONOMY_ASTRAL_LEVERAGE_AND_ROUTING_V0.md), AMO-Q101).

**Imperfection is not collapse.** An imperfectly deployed Leaf does not automatically trigger Replacement, premature retreat or a new Emergence, and scarring alone never triggers them (AMO-D096, AMO-D114, L56). Equally, stabilization at a sufficiently functional compromised state is a **canonical outcome** in which no routing question ever arises.

## 8. No second charge for the same developmental damage

A damaged Emergence can produce two distinct consequences, and they are not one value:

1. **Tuber-side sunk construction** spent before Full Deployment (AMO-D109, AMO-D110);
2. **a compromised mature Leaf** after Full Deployment.

Both follow from one developmental history. After the boundary, the Tuber is **not** charged again merely because the Leaf began mature life imperfect: the cost of imperfect construction is already embodied in that spent construction and in the reduced quality of the manifestation it produced. There is no standing per-moment persistent loss for existing in an imperfect state, and mature damage never directly debits equivalent Tuber integrity, reserves or Developmental Maturity (AMO-D111, L55).

Persistent Tuber consequence remains **downstream**. It requires an actual biological path through function, opportunity, condition and the [Pathological Tuber Impact](21_PATHOLOGICAL_TUBER_IMPACT_V0.md) boundary (AMO-D090–AMO-D092, AMO-Q116, AMO-Q118). Direct harmful exposure to the Tuber itself remains a separate route and not a shortcut from mature Leaf imperfection (AMO-D090).

## 9. Astral handoff

Existing biology ↔ magic rules stay authoritative ([20](20_BIOLOGY_MAGIC_BOUNDARY_AND_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md)). Neither inference is permitted: an imperfect mature Leaf is not therefore un-inhabitable, and Full Deployment is not therefore Astral Ready (AMO-D107). A compromised but biologically valid mature manifestation may become inhabitable if the separate access and readiness requirements are met; the readiness condition itself remains open (AMO-Q085, AMO-Q112, AMO-Q115).

**Playability does not require biological perfection, and it does not erase impairment.** If the Warden inhabits this Leaf, its scars, Structural Integrity, Functional Capacity and developmental history remain exactly as biology left them. Normal biological simulation pauses while embodied; astral exit and re-entry repair nothing (AMO-D072, AMO-D084, AMO-D086, L49, L50). How manifestation impairment changes a playable Amorpho is AMO-Q109 and is not designed here.

## 10. Output contract

The trace ends with an updated mature Leaf state, described conceptually:

- **Structural Integrity** — the current mature physical state.
- **Leaf Functional Capacity** — the useful biological work it can still potentially perform.
- **Mature stability direction** — improving, stable or declining.
- **Attainable-ceiling note**, where useful — a qualitative observation that developmental damage may limit what this manifestation can regain.

These are descriptions, not required stored fields, meters or percentages. No new variable, scalar or structural-to-functional function is introduced.

## 11. Worked traces

**A — favorable stabilization.** Abstract Individual A's Leaf reaches Full Deployment imperfectly; structural defects remain and Functional Capacity is below what an undamaged equivalent would have offered. Subsequent Fit becomes favorable and A's condition can support a response. The Leaf stabilizes, and Functional Capacity improves through surviving structure while the defects remain visible. A useful mature manifestation continues; no routing threshold is approached. **Stop:** what it can now do, not what the phase ultimately yields.

**B — stable compromised state.** The same imperfect deployment meets merely adequate Fit and no further damage. Structural Integrity remains compromised and Functional Capacity remains reduced but viable. The state stabilizes and the active phase continues. **Stable is not recovered**, and it is not failure either.

**C — further decline.** After imperfect deployment, Fit remains poor. Inherited weakness translates into worsening function and reduced stability. The Leaf approaches a point at which future replacement or retreat routing could become relevant. **Stop** before that decision ([25](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md), AMO-Q101).

**D — additional mature damage.** A Leaf that began mature life imperfect later sustains a new mature-state event. It is processed through the mature event-and-impairment model from A's **actual** current state ([23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md)). Inherited developmental impairment remains part of the history; current state reflects both origins. No direct Tuber debit follows from the new damage.

**E — astral embodiment.** The imperfect mature Leaf meets the separate readiness and access requirements and the Warden inhabits it. The biological state persists unchanged through embodiment and exit. **Stop** before any combat or capability mapping (AMO-Q109).

## 12. Deferred rules and acceptance

AMO-Q086 owns how inherited developmental impairment determines initial mature Structural Integrity and Functional Capacity, the structural-to-functional mapping, and susceptibility to later events. AMO-Q103 owns mature stabilization and recovery extent, rates, condition and environmental dependence, and any attainable functional ceiling. AMO-Q101 owns the functional-collapse criterion and subsequent routing; AMO-Q084 owns route topology. AMO-Q116 owns active-phase productivity; AMO-Q118 owns the crossing into actual persistent loss. AMO-Q112 and AMO-Q115 own Astral Readiness and Signal behaviour; AMO-Q109 owns playable consequences. No new question, decision, law, variable or mechanism is introduced by this trace.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Imperfect mature start | Mature state begins from the actual deployed condition, without normalization (AMO-D113). |
| Favorable conditions | Stabilization and functional improvement are possible; structural defects may remain. |
| Adequate conditions | A stable compromised state is a legitimate outcome, not a failure to heal. |
| Poor conditions | Inherited weakness may worsen function and stability. |
| Tuber accounting | Construction was already paid; imperfection alone creates no second or standing Tuber charge (AMO-D111, L55). |
| New mature damage | Handled by the mature impairment model from the actual current state; origins stay distinct. |
| Astral access | Readiness is independent of perfection in both directions; embodiment resets no biology. |
| Routing | Only sufficiently declined mature function makes routing relevant; imperfection does not. |

**Result:** a Leaf handed over imperfectly is a valid mature manifestation. Under favorable conditions it can stabilize and regain useful function without rebuilding what was never built; under poor conditions inherited weakness can worsen toward a later routing question. Neither outcome charges the Tuber a second time for the same developmental damage.
