# 48 — Roster Timing and One-Consciousness Biological Opportunity Trace, v0

**Status:** integration and gameplay-dilemma trace of accepted rules, not a new design. It follows one Warden and three persistent individuals through one threat and asks whether the accepted biology already makes *which Amorpho do I inhabit now?* a meaningful decision. It defines no value, rate, duration, combat stat, kit, species fact, mission, interface, notification or mechanic, and it adds no decision, law or question.

> **One Consciousness turns biological timing into roster strategy.**

## 1. Purpose

[45](45_REMAINING_PRODUCTIVE_OPPORTUNITY_QUALITATIVE_COMPOSITION_V0.md) §16 recorded that choosing an Amorpho to inhabit also means choosing whose biological opportunity to interrupt. [46](46_DORMANCY_COMMITMENT_BOUNDARY_V0.md) and [47](47_DEEP_DORMANCY_DURATION_AND_ACTIVE_PHASE_REACTIVATION_BOUNDARY_V0.md) then closed the lifecycle at both ends. This trace tests the claim in play:

> Does the accepted biology naturally make "Which Amorpho do I inhabit now?" a strategic decision — not only *which fighter is strongest*, but *whose current biological opportunity am I willing to interrupt*?

The test fails if the dilemma only appears after adding a cooldown, stamina ticket, availability timer, scripted penalty, species balancing bonus, numeric productivity value or combat-stat comparison. The trace uses **omniscient design-level knowledge**; what the Warden can actually perceive is a separate question (§12).

## 2. The Warden constraint

One Warden, one persistent human body, **one consciousness and one direct presence** (AMO-D028, AMO-D029, AMO-D101). While inhabiting one individual the human body stays where it was left, and the Warden has no simultaneous presence anywhere else — no parallel control, no strategic presence with a dormant Tuber, no tag-team.

Assumed for the trace, and not decided by it: A and B each carry an Astral Anchor, the Warden's Astral Capacity sustains both connections, both pass the three gates, and both are within whatever transfer reach eventually exists (AMO-D063, AMO-D067, AMO-Q040, AMO-Q082).

## 3. The three individuals

No species, no values. Everything below is lifecycle strategy and current state only.

**Individual A — a short, valuable window.** A strongly seasonal, concentrated-opportunity strategy. A mature viable Leaf, rooted, with good Functional Capacity and favourable Fit, in a short and biologically valuable active window. A is in **Dormancy Approach, not committed** ([46](46_DORMANCY_COMMITMENT_BOUNDARY_V0.md) §2). All three components of Remaining Productive Opportunity align, and Productive Return is occurring now. A is inhabitable, and embodying it now would interrupt one of the most valuable periods of its cycle.
*Character:* concentrated opportunity, strong potential return during the window, then meaningful dormancy. Its limitation is **timing**, not quality; seasonal strategy is not inferior uptime (AMO-D147).

**Individual B — extended-growth availability.** A broadly available active strategy. A mature viable Leaf, inhabitable, in good enough condition, in a suitable environment. Productive Opportunity exists, and Biological Growth Availability is expected to stay open substantially longer than A's — less temporally concentrated.
*Character:* **flexibility**. Embodying B still interrupts Productive Return, rooted recovery where relevant, and current opportunity.

**Individual C — dormant, nearing reactivation.** A persistent Tuber in Deep Dormancy: no playable manifestation, Astrally Silent, intrinsically approaching Reactivation Readiness, its environment becoming suitable, its core plausibly able to fund a future Emergence ([47](47_DEEP_DORMANCY_DURATION_AND_ACTIVE_PHASE_REACTIVATION_BOUNDARY_V0.md) §4–§6). **C is not a playable option.**
*Character:* C is valuable and strategically relevant while unavailable, because its biology has not yet produced a body. **C is not on cooldown**: it is a living dormant individual, the Warden cannot force it awake, good conditions cannot instantly produce a fighter, and no timer exists (AMO-D158–AMO-D160).

## 4. The threat

One abstract external threat appears, important enough that the Warden wants an Amorpho body. No mission is defined. Manifestation destruction is not assumed, and the ordinary resolution families — surrender, escape, conscious withdrawal, objective resolution — remain available ([36](36_COMBAT_RESOLUTION_SURRENDER_ESCAPE_AND_WITHDRAWAL_V0.md), AMO-D124–AMO-D127).

## 5. The choice set

```
WARDEN OWNS A, B, C

A: short valuable productive window · approaching Dormancy · inhabitable now
B: extended-growth availability · inhabitable now · broader future opportunity
C: Deep Dormancy · nearing reactivation · not playable

                    THREAT APPEARS
                          ▼
        WARDEN MUST CHOOSE ONE DIRECT BODY
                          ▼
CHOOSING A   high biological opportunity cost; possibly the stronger tactical choice
CHOOSING B   lower immediate timing cost; still a real biological cost
C            cannot be forced available
```

The immediate choice is **A or B**. C shapes planning and cannot be selected because the Warden wants it. Not embodying either is also a choice (§10, Branch C).

## 6. Branch A — the Warden inhabits A

1. A is rooted and productively active.
2. The Warden inhabits A.
3. A's Productive Return pauses, and its lifecycle progression pauses with the rest of its ordinary biology (AMO-D136, AMO-D157).
4. World Time continues. A's valuable external opportunity keeps passing (AMO-D145).
5. The threat is addressed; the encounter resolves without manifestation destruction.
6. The Warden remains embodied for some time, then roots A again (AMO-D032).
7. Biology resumes. Some of A's short, high-value opportunity has been **permanently missed** — not banked, not repaid.

No pathology, no penalty, no damage beyond whatever the encounter really did (AMO-D148). A's lifecycle did not advance while embodied, so A resumes still in Approach — but in a World that has moved on.

**One spatial consequence falls out without being designed.** The embodied Amorpho carries its own core (AMO-D129), and exit roots the individual where it stands (AMO-D032). If the threat is elsewhere, A goes to it — and after resolution the Warden must either root A wherever the encounter ended, possibly in worse Fit, or stay embodied longer to walk it back, spending more of the window. **Choosing a body also chooses where that body will be when its biology resumes.**

**Choosing A may still be right.** The threat may justify it for reasons outside this trace. *Biological opportunity cost is a strategic cost, not a moral judgement.*

## 7. Branch B — the Warden inhabits B

1. B is rooted; the Warden embodies it.
2. B's Productive Return pauses. World Time continues.
3. The encounter resolves; the Warden later roots B.
4. Some opportunity was lost — rooted work during embodiment, external opportunity that passed, any recovery time.
5. B's lifecycle remains broadly active-capable, and suitable future opportunity remains.
6. B resumes productive work with a **less severe timing consequence** than A would have suffered.

**B is not free.** Extended growth is not free embodiment: B pays every cost A pays. The difference is only that **its lifecycle may offer more future opportunity afterward** ([45](45_REMAINING_PRODUCTIVE_OPPORTUNITY_QUALITATIVE_COMPOSITION_V0.md) §11). The same spatial consequence applies.

Meanwhile **A, unselected, keeps living**: rooted, biologically running, converting its concentrated window into Productive Return.

## 8. A versus B

| | **A** | **B** |
|---|---|---|
| What embodiment interrupts | concentrated, high-value opportunity in a short window | broader opportunity that remains available for longer |
| What it still costs | paused return, passing opportunity, relocation away from its site | the same kinds of cost |
| What remains afterwards | possibly little of the window | possibly much |
| Why choose it anyway | tactical fit, Core security, location, the threat's importance | flexibility |

The Warden is choosing between **different biological timing costs**, not between *available* and *on cooldown*. That is the desired result.

**The strongest body is not automatically the cheapest body to use.** A may eventually be tactically excellent and sit in its most valuable window; B may be less suited tactically and far cheaper to interrupt. The reverse also holds: B may be biologically cheap and tactically inadequate for this threat, which can make risking A rational. Nothing here computes which; **no universally correct body emerges from biology alone**.

Two existing dimensions stack on top without changing the shape: **Core security** — what destruction would expose in each (AMO-D131, AMO-D144) — and **recovery status**, where a rehabilitating Leaf's recovery would also pause (AMO-D139, AMO-D141). A future **Bloom** candidate would add a signalled core and a different risk (L63). None is needed for the main trace.

## 9. C during and after the threat

While the Warden handles the threat, **C stays dormant on its own biology**; World Time and dormant biology run together (AMO-D158). The Warden cannot remotely order C to emerge, use C as an emergency body before a manifestation exists, or put consciousness into bare-Tuber locomotion — Deep Dormancy is closed and the Tuber is not a playable form (AMO-D060, AMO-D099).

Later, while the Warden is elsewhere, C may develop readiness. If its environment is suitable and funding is feasible, Reactivation Commitment, Pre-Emergence and Emergence proceed **autonomously**, without Warden presence (AMO-D097, AMO-D160).

**Emergence is not instant roster availability.** The Tuber funds construction, Emergence takes biological time, Full Deployment must occur before normal inhabitation, and Astral Readiness is a separate magical state ([29](29_REPLACEMENT_EMERGENCE_TO_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md), AMO-D106, AMO-D107, AMO-D086). C becomes a possible body **gradually, through biology** — never *cooldown complete → character unlocked*.

**The Warden can miss C's timing too.** Because unattended individuals live, C may emerge and begin its own rooted productive phase while the Warden is inhabiting someone else — and the Warden then meets another timing choice, now about C. No notification is designed.

## 10. Further branches

**Branch C — do not embody immediately.** The Warden embodies neither A nor B: Human action, avoidance, accepting the conflict's consequence, or waiting for C may each be possible depending on context, and none is designed here (AMO-Q130, AMO-Q028). The point is narrow: **the roster architecture never forces the Warden to consume biological opportunity.** The conflict may still have consequences.

**Branch D — use A briefly.** A is inhabited, the conflict resolves quickly, and the Warden roots again while substantial opportunity remains. Some was interrupted; much remains. **Embodiment duration matters without any timer**, because the World kept moving only for as long as the Warden stayed.

**Branch E — B held too long.** B is inhabited and kept embodied far longer than the threat required. Return stays paused, conditions eventually change, and substantial opportunity is lost. **Extended growth is flexibility, not permission.**

**Branch F — A commits after return.** A is used, rooted again, and resumes with a short remainder; later it crosses Dormancy Commitment. Relocation cannot then recover what was missed, and favourable conditions do not reopen the phase ([46](46_DORMANCY_COMMITMENT_BOUNDARY_V0.md), L65, L68).

**Branch G — C changes the roster.** C completes dormancy and reactivation, its Emergence succeeds, Full Deployment occurs and Astral Readiness allows entry. Future choices now range over A, B and C **as their current biology stands**. The roster is dynamic, and no static character-select assumption survives.

**Branch H — the cost moves through the cycle.** Suppose the Warden chose B, and A — unselected and rooted — later crosses its own Dormancy Commitment while its Leaf still exists in Senescence. Its Biological Growth Availability has closed, so embodying A **no longer interrupts productive opportunity**; it pauses only A's retirement, while World conditions continue (AMO-D157). The same individual that was the most expensive body in its window may be among the cheapest once its window has closed. No rule produced that; the lifecycle did. Whether a delayed retirement into a later, harsher World carries its own consequence belongs to existing owners (AMO-Q102, AMO-Q117).

## 11. Integration findings

**One Consciousness creates real scarcity.** One threat and several viable bodies give exactly one direct presence. The scarce resource is not an energy point; it is **Warden direct presence**. Owning more individuals therefore does not simply mean *more fighters is always better*: it also means more simultaneous biological windows, more conflicting opportunities, and one consciousness unable to inhabit all of them. No management-burden system is needed to produce that; it follows from AMO-D028.

**Unselected individuals keep living.** While A or B is inhabited, every other individual continues its own biology. The World does not pause the roster ([09](09_EMBODIMENT_AND_ASTRAL_TRANSFER.md) §9, AMO-D009). That is a central source of scheduling pressure.

**Ownership is not playability.** The Warden owns all three individuals throughout — in Deep Dormancy, Emergence, rooted Leaf, embodiment and Senescence — and only some of those phases permit embodiment (AMO-D065, AMO-D073). *An owned Amorpho may be biologically alive and strategically important without being currently playable.*

**No reset between encounters.** After the threat, A and B keep their actual interrupted opportunity, biological state, any damage and their lifecycle position; C keeps its dormancy history. There is no rematch reset.

**Today's decisions make tomorrow's roster.** The roster available later is partly shaped by what the Warden did now — through interrupted return, damage, recovery, lifecycle timing, dormancy and emergence, never through timers. *The roster evolves because the plants live, not because cooldown timers expire.*

**Strategy produces playstyle before combat does.** A seasonal individual and an extended-growth individual already make the Warden behave differently — guard one window, spend the other's flexibility — with no combat move defined. *Seasonal and extended-growth strategies create different decisions without artificial balancing.* It is acceptable that B will often be biologically cheaper to use than A; future body and combat differentiation is the intended counter-pressure, and nothing here penalizes B to compensate.

**Dormancy reads as planning, not punishment.** C's unavailability is biologically meaningful, qualitatively foreseeable and strategically relevant, without any lockout rule in the architecture. No claim about player experience is made.

## 12. Ambiguities exposed

No contradiction was found, and no decision, law or question was required. Two places where the dilemma leans on something still open were recorded against their existing owners:

- **Moving presence between bodies.** Exiting B mid-conflict to take up A would root B where it stands (AMO-D032) and require establishing presence in A (AMO-D101) — never parallel, never a tag-team. Whether direct presence can pass from one Amorpho to another or must first return to the human body is not decided; it affects how expensive a mid-conflict switch is. Recorded under **AMO-Q040**.
- **What the Warden can know.** The dilemma is a real decision only to the extent the Warden can judge A's lifecycle position, the opportunity each individual has left and how near C is to reactivation. This trace used design-level omniscience. Simulation truth and player knowledge are already separate (AMO-D051); the information model for lifecycle position, opportunity and dormant readiness is recorded under **AMO-Q044**, alongside AMO-Q119 and AMO-Q111. Nothing is designed.

**Result:** the dilemma is already there. Nothing had to be added to make *which body?* hard: one consciousness, a moving World, plants that keep living and lifecycles that differ were enough. What the trace cannot supply is the other half of the scale — *why this body would be better in this fight* — which is the part of the game still undesigned. [49](49_COMBAT_PILLARS_AND_BIOLOGICAL_ROSTER_STRATEGY_V0.md) supplies that counter-pressure qualitatively: this trace establishes **Biological Deployment Cost**, and doc 49 establishes **Combat Suitability** as a separate axis (AMO-D161).
