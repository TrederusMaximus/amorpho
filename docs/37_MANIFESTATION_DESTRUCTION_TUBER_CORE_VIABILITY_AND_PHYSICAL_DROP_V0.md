# 37 — Manifestation Destruction, Tuber Core Viability and Physical Drop, v0

**Status:** conceptual physical-ontology and outcome pass using owner-supplied design direction. It follows the exceptional case where a manifestation is physically destroyed ([36](36_COMBAT_RESOLUTION_SURRENDER_ESCAPE_AND_WITHDRAWAL_V0.md), AMO-D127) and establishes what physically remains. It defines no combat value, damage figure, Tuber size, viability threshold, productivity formula, inventory, pickup, theft, ownership-transfer, rescue-mission or respawn mechanic, and it researches no botany.

> **The Tuber rides inside the Amorpho as its core. Destroy the body and what remains is only what the core had already become — lying where it fell.**

## 1. Purpose

[35](35_COMBAT_PROTECTION_MANIFESTATION_BODY_DAMAGE_AND_BIOLOGICAL_PERSISTENCE_V0.md) established that body-reaching damage is biological, and [36](36_COMBAT_RESOLUTION_SURRENDER_ESCAPE_AND_WITHDRAWAL_V0.md) established that ordinary encounters resolve before destruction. This pass answers what the escalated case actually produces, and it answers a physical question the architecture had left implicit: **where is the persistent Tuber while the Amorpho is walking around the World?**

## 2. Physical ontology of the embodied Amorpho

> **When a Leaf or Bloom is astrally inhabited and becomes the mobile Amorpho body, the persistent Tuber is physically carried within that body as its central biological core.**

The Tuber is not left behind at the previous rooting site, and the Amorpho is not a remote projection of a stationary underground plant. The temporary manifestation is the body **around** the persistent core. Describing that core as the Amorpho's heart is acceptable as imagery; it is not an anatomical redefinition. The Tuber remains what it has always been: the persistent biological body of the individual (AMO-D058, AMO-D095).

```text
PROTECTION                shields, armour, guard, evasion (AMO-D122)
   ↓
MANIFESTATION BODY        the temporary Leaf or Bloom (AMO-D120)
   ↓
CENTRAL PERSISTENT CORE   the Tuber, carried inside it
```

The layering is physical, and it does not change any damage rule. Ordinary body-reaching damage damages the **manifestation**; it is not automatic Tuber injury, and the Tuber is never a combat health pool (AMO-D121, L55, L58). Direct Tuber injury remains its own extreme pathway (AMO-D090). **Core exposure** is named here only as a physical concept the layering makes expressible; no hit location, body zone or core-targeting mechanic is designed (AMO-Q109, AMO-Q117).

## 3. Movement and rooting move the individual

Because the core travels inside the body, an embodied Amorpho that walks, travels, flees, explores, fights or relocates **physically moves the persistent individual**. When the Warden exits and the Amorpho roots, the Tuber is physically present at that location and ordinary rooted biology resumes there.

This formalises what strategic establishment already assumed: a player may inhabit an Amorpho and move it across the world precisely in order to establish that individual somewhere chosen ([09](09_EMBODIMENT_AND_ASTRAL_TRANSFER.md) §9, AMO-D054). No rooting animation, placement rule or transport abstraction is designed.

## 4. Manifestation destruction exposes the core

> **Destruction removes the temporary body and exposes whatever persistent Tuber actually remains. Nothing respawns.**

```text
EMBODIED AMORPHO
   ↓  manifestation body physically destroyed
LEAF OR BLOOM MANIFESTATION ENDS        embodiment ends with it (AMO-D118, L57)
   ↓
PERSISTENT CORE EXPOSED
   ↓
WHATEVER SURVIVES REMAINS WHERE THE AMORPHO FELL
```

This is physical continuity, not a loot-generation event. Explicitly rejected: a Tuber that teleports home, a replacement Tuber appearing in an inventory, an automatic return to the previous pot, and any assignment of the remains to whoever won the fight. **Location matters**, and who reaches the site is a separate event from the encounter that created it.

What is exposed is the *actual* individual, with its identity, genetics, lineage, Developmental Maturity, Current Biological Condition, any prior Pathological Tuber Impact, its seasonal history and whatever biological capacity it currently holds. Nothing is regenerated, rounded up or reset for the occasion (AMO-D011, AMO-D022, AMO-D080).

## 5. Tuber Core Viability

> **Tuber Core Viability:** if the current manifested body were completely lost now, could the remaining Tuber continue as a living persistent individual?

It is a **qualitative biological evaluation, not a meter**. No threshold, value or formula is defined, and the criterion itself belongs to the existing death-condition question (AMO-Q104, AMO-Q102). Two outcomes follow destruction:

| Outcome | What remains | What follows |
|---|---|---|
| **Viable core** | the Tuber, alive but immobile, plus its Astral Anchor, at the collapse site | physical rescue is possible; re-rooting can resume ordinary biology |
| **Non-viable core** | no living individual; the Anchor may remain as a physical object | the persistent biological individual is **lost** |

So: **a manifestation can be lost while the individual survives, or manifestation destruction can end the individual if no viable core remains.** That second outcome is categorically different from ordinary Leaf loss, and it is the one genuinely irreversible consequence combat can reach.

**Viable does not mean healthy.** A surviving core may be depleted, damaged, pathologically affected, poorly buffered and badly in need of favourable re-rooting. Viability only means it can continue as an individual.

This is not ordinary **Functional Collapse**, which concerns failure of the active Leaf *role* ([33](33_MATURE_LEAF_FUNCTIONAL_COLLAPSE_BOUNDARY_V0.md)). This pass concerns physical destruction of the body and the viability of the core underneath. The two can interact — accumulated damage may produce either — but they are not the same concept, and a biologically terminal Leaf ending under the ordinary lifecycle leaves the Tuber in place rather than exposed in the field ([34](34_EMBODIMENT_ACROSS_TERMINAL_MANIFESTATION_COLLAPSE_V0.md)).

## 6. The season decides what is left

The core carried inside the Amorpho is **not constant across the season**. The Tuber funded the manifestation's construction, and an active Leaf may later contribute biological work back toward the persistent Tuber ([30](30_EMERGENCE_INVESTMENT_AND_FULL_DEPLOYMENT_ACCOUNTING_BOUNDARY_V0.md), AMO-D081, AMO-D109). Destruction therefore exposes a different core depending on when it happens:

| History so far | The core may be | The body may be |
|---|---|---|
| **Inhabited soon after Full Deployment** | recently depleted by construction, not yet rebuilt, at high total-loss risk | fresh, minimally scarred, unweathered — potentially an excellent fighter |
| **Inhabited early, then held embodied through many fights** | still depleted, because embodiment suspended the rebuilding | heavily battle-worn |
| **Rooted and productive first, then inhabited** | better provisioned, more robust, more likely to survive body loss | worn by weather, pests or incident |
| **Rooted in exceptionally protected conditions, then inhabited** | better provisioned | still in good condition |

That inversion is the point: **manifestation quality and core security can move in opposite directions**, and no single visual trajectory is mandatory. A pristine, newly deployed Amorpho may be one of the most biologically dangerous bodies to risk completely, while a battered late-season one may sit on a robust core.

The **biological pause** is what makes the early case particularly dangerous. A Warden who inhabits soon after Full Deployment and stays embodied through many encounters suspends ordinary productivity throughout, so the core is never rebuilt while the body accumulates combat damage: the fighter becomes visibly worn **without the core becoming any safer** (AMO-D117, AMO-D123).

A depleted core is still the core. Even immediately after costly construction the individual has a persistent Tuber; what may be missing is an **independently viable survivor** if the body is catastrophically destroyed. The ontology is unchanged — the Tuber never stops being the persistent biological body (AMO-D058, AMO-D129).

Neither direction is guaranteed. Waiting does not promise a robust core — poor Environmental Fit, prior damage, weak Leaf function or pathology can leave a later-season Tuber worse than expected — and a well-protected Leaf may remain excellent late (L51). **Viability is history-dependent, never a calendar rule:** no `day X of season` test may be introduced. What that history is made of is specified in [39](39_ACTIVE_LEAF_PRODUCTIVE_RETURN_AND_PERSISTENT_TUBER_BENEFIT_V0.md) — **Active Leaf Productive Return**, which accrues only while the individual is rooted and biologically running, so an inhabited period rebuilds nothing (AMO-D135, AMO-D136). Realized return is never erased by the loss that follows (AMO-D137). Everything quantitative remains AMO-Q116.

**Destruction ends the manifestation, not the season's history.** Biological work already performed remains part of the persistent outcome; the Tuber is never rolled back to its pre-season state.

## 7. One body, two modes — and what appearance does not show

**Rooting and embodiment do not create two Leaf or Bloom bodies.** The same manifestation persists across the rooted biological form and the Warden-inhabited mobile form, so its physical history is continuous in both directions (AMO-D030, AMO-D120, AMO-D134):

- damage the rooted Leaf already carries — weather, pests, mechanical incident, scars, any existing impairment — is still there when the Warden later inhabits it;
- damage the embodied Amorpho takes in combat is still there when embodiment ends and rooted biology resumes.

There is no conversion step, no reset and no separate fighter copy. This is the same continuity [35](35_COMBAT_PROTECTION_MANIFESTATION_BODY_DAMAGE_AND_BIOLOGICAL_PERSISTENCE_V0.md) established for damage, stated as physical identity of the body itself.

> **The body tells you what happened to the manifestation; it does not necessarily tell you what survives inside it.**

Visible condition records the **manifestation's** history. It does not reveal current core capacity, Tuber Core Viability, how much productive return the season has yielded, or what could be salvaged after destruction. Two Amorphos may look broadly alike and differ completely in what destroying them would expose:

| | Manifestation | Core |
|---|---|---|
| **Long productive rooted life, inhabited later** | visibly worn by weather, pests or incident | potentially robust and viable |
| **Inhabited early, worn down by combat** | visibly worn by battle | potentially severely depleted, possibly with no viable survivor |

So none of these inferences is permitted: *same visible damage → same remains*, *scarred fighter → valuable core*, *worn fighter → depleted core*. The persistent core has its own biological history, and **damage source cannot be read off appearance either** — wear, pest damage, intermittent combat, prolonged embodied use or any combination can look similar.

One exception is not about appearance at all: **manifestation type may itself carry biological information.** A Bloom exists only because the persistent individual had reached substantial capability, so its presence is a lower-bound signal about the core that no inspection of a Leaf can supply — while still revealing nothing exact, and generating no special remains ([41](41_BLOOM_EMBODIMENT_CORE_SECURITY_AND_DESTRUCTION_VALUE_V0.md), AMO-D142, AMO-D144). Leaf appearance remains weak evidence either way.

The result is **natural information asymmetry**. An observer may perceive visible manifestation damage, protection condition where that is perceptible, and obvious terminal states, while exact core security stays hidden. A pristine, apparently freshly deployed Amorpho is genuinely informative — it suggests little rooted active time, a body near its fresh condition, and therefore high present body quality with potentially poor post-destruction core security — but appearance is a **signal, not proof**: a well-protected later-season manifestation may still look excellent. An opponent facing two similarly battered Amorphos may not know whether destroying them would expose a valuable viable core, a marginal rescue case, or nothing living at all. No scanning, inspection, readout or interface mechanic is designed here (AMO-Q111, AMO-Q109).

## 8. Eligibility and deployment risk are different questions

> **When *can* the Warden inhabit?** — Full Deployment, plus the ordinary access and Astral Readiness gates (AMO-D063, AMO-D107). Unchanged.
>
> **When *should* the Warden risk this individual?** — a strategic judgement informed by Tuber Core Viability.

These must not collapse. Core viability governs the **consequences** of total body loss, not permission to embody, and **no lockout** may be introduced on the grounds that a core is too depleted unless existing biology independently requires it. The interesting case is precisely that embodiment may be permitted while total-loss risk is extreme, and the player decides whether that is acceptable.

Three related but distinct considerations follow, kept as concepts rather than statistics:

- **Fighter quality** — how good this body currently is to use;
- **Core security** — how likely the individual is to survive complete body loss;
- **Capture value** — how much viable biological value could physically be recovered from the site afterwards.

Early deployment may favour fighter quality while leaving little worth capturing and risking everything; later deployment may favour core security while accepting a worn body and leaving a more valuable remainder if it falls. Late destruction can therefore leave something genuinely worth recovering, and early destruction may leave an opponent nothing living at all — **an emergent anti-capture trade-off, not a self-destruct mechanic**, which is why none is added. No economics, rarity tier or optimum is defined, and no deployment choice is labelled correct.

## 9. The Astral Anchor travels with the core

The canonical term is the **Astral Anchor**: a physical, reusable object associated with the persistent individual rather than with a pot, property or place (AMO-D061, AMO-Q089). This pass makes its physical home explicit:

> The Anchor is physically with the **persistent Tuber core**, not fixed to the temporary Leaf surface. It therefore travels with the Tuber into the embodied Amorpho, through movement, and through the loss of the manifestation.

Two consequences:

- **Continuity across manifestations is automatic.** The old Leaf ends, the Tuber persists with its Anchor, a later Emergence builds a new Leaf, and the same Anchor is still with the individual. No Anchor needs to appear with each new manifestation.
- **The Anchor may outlive the plant.** If destruction leaves no viable core, the individual is gone and the physical Anchor may still remain at the site as an object. That is the canonical extreme remnant.

An Anchor is **not** the individual: it mediates access, and its survival preserves nothing biological. An empty Anchor is an object, not a plant, and whether Anchors can be damaged, rebound, reused, inherited or destroyed remains open (AMO-Q090, AMO-Q091). Carrying one's own attached Anchor while the individual relocates is not attaching, removing or reallocating it, so the human-hands rule is untouched (AMO-D062, L42).

## 10. Rescue, custody and what comes next

An exposed Tuber is **not playable**. It cannot walk, flee, fight or carry itself anywhere, and the Warden cannot pilot it home: the Tuber is never a locomotion or combat form (AMO-D089, AMO-D099). It may be astrally **reachable** under the existing Astral Window rules, and reachability is not playability — moving it requires another physical actor (AMO-D099, [28](28_ASTRAL_WINDOW_END_TO_END_TRACE_V0.md)). If no viable core survives, there is no living individual for a Window to operate on at all.

Physical rescue therefore means someone must pick the Tuber up, carry it, transport it and place or root it somewhere suitable. Candidate actors include the Human Warden travelling to the site (AMO-Q046, AMO-Q130) and a later, sequentially embodied Amorpho returning to recover it (AMO-Q047) — the latter breaks nothing, because One Consciousness only forbids simultaneous bodies (AMO-D028, L22). No interaction, carrying rule or mission is designed.

Because the remains are physically in the world, **another actor may reach them first**, and the architecture must not assume only the owner can interact with them. That is where the existing boundary does the work: picking a Tuber up establishes **physical custody**, which is not ownership, not the Warden bond, not astral authorisation and not a lineage rewrite (AMO-D065, L43). Theft, capture and ownership rules remain future design (AMO-Q008, AMO-Q091, AMO-Q093).

Rescue guarantees nothing. A recovered Tuber continues from its actual persistent state, remains biologically simulated while unrooted — *a Tuber does not become timeless because someone is carrying it* (AMO-D093, AMO-Q117) — and once rooted resumes ordinary life and routing: Replacement where feasible, consolidation, Dormancy, or a later cycle ([25](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md), AMO-D096, AMO-Q101). **A dropped Tuber is not a respawn token:** Replacement still depends on capacity, remaining opportunity and routing, and an early survivor may have opportunity without capacity while a late survivor may have capacity without much reason to spend it. Nothing about post-rescue recovery or routing is resolved here (AMO-Q102, AMO-Q105).

The collapse location consequently matters in itself. A body destroyed near home, deep in hostile terrain or far out on an expedition produces very different rescue problems. No territory or control system follows.

## 11. Worked traces

**A — early catastrophic loss.** The Tuber funds emergence; the Leaf reaches Full Deployment; the Warden inhabits it soon after with a fresh, high-quality body. Little of the season has yet rebuilt the core. The Warden escalates to manifestation destruction. The body is destroyed, the core is exposed, and it is **non-viable**: the biological individual is lost. The Astral Anchor remains at the site. No rescue is possible, because nothing living remains to rescue.

**B — mid-season viable rescue.** The Leaf has done meaningful biological work and the core has become viable. Destruction occurs. Tuber and Anchor remain at the site; embodiment ends and the Warden returns to the human body. The Tuber cannot move itself. Another actor later recovers it, transports it and roots it, and biology resumes from its actual state.

**C — late-season destruction.** The Leaf has completed most of a successful active period and shows the wear of it, while the core is robust. Destruction takes the fighter. A substantial viable core remains, the season's completed work remains part of its history, and rescue and re-rooting may preserve the individual. Whether Replacement is worth attempting is a routing question left open.

**D — someone else reaches it first.** After destruction, a viable Tuber and its Anchor lie at the site. The original Warden is back in the human body. Another physical actor arrives first and may take **custody** if future world rules permit. Ownership, bond and astral authorisation do not change by being picked up. **Stop** — no theft system.

**E — a later manifestation.** A rescued core is re-rooted, biology continues, the lifecycle eventually permits a new Emergence, a new Leaf reaches Full Deployment, and the same persistent individual is playable again with the same Anchor. That took biological time and lifecycle progression; no respawn shortcut existed.

**F — early tactical exposure.** A Warden deliberately deploys a pristine early-season Amorpho, accepting weak core security for a strong body, and the fight escalates to destruction. No viable core survives, so an opponent can recover no living plant from the site — only the Anchor. A tactical possibility, described as neither optimal nor admirable.

## 12. Deferred mechanics and acceptance

AMO-Q104 owns the viability criterion, with AMO-Q102 for recovery dynamics and AMO-Q094 for fragment-survival identity. AMO-Q116 owns how seasonal productive work becomes persistent capacity, and remains **fully open**. AMO-Q117 owns exposed and transported Tuber condition and direct susceptibility; AMO-Q109 and AMO-Q026 own the combat side, including any core exposure or direct-core pathway; AMO-Q089, AMO-Q090 and AMO-Q091 own Anchor form, destructibility and rebinding; AMO-Q046, AMO-Q047 and AMO-Q130 own rescue actors; AMO-Q008 and AMO-Q093 own theft, custody and ownership; AMO-Q101 and AMO-Q084 own later routing; AMO-Q105 owns treatment. No new question was required.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Tuber location | An embodied Amorpho carries the persistent Tuber; movement moves the individual (AMO-D129). |
| Rooting | Rooting establishes the Tuber physically at the new location. |
| Ordinary body hit | Damages the manifestation; it is not automatic Tuber injury (AMO-D121, L58). |
| Complete destruction | The core is exposed at the collapse site; nothing respawns or is generated (AMO-D130). |
| Viable core | A non-playable, immobile Tuber remains and requires physical rescue. |
| Non-viable core | The persistent biological individual is lost (AMO-D131). |
| Anchor continuity | The Anchor travels with the core and survives ordinary manifestation loss (AMO-D132). |
| Empty Anchor | It may remain alone when no individual survives; an Anchor is not a plant. |
| No automatic drop assignment | The winner receives nothing automatically; who reaches the site is a separate event. |
| Custody | Pickup is custody, never ownership, bond or astral authorisation (AMO-D065). |
| Season timing | Early and late destruction may leave very different cores; no calendar rule. |
| One body | Rooted and embodied are the same manifestation; damage carries both ways with no conversion or reset (AMO-D134). |
| Appearance | Visible condition records manifestation history, never core viability; similar bodies may hide different cores. |
| Information | A pristine body is a signal, not proof; core security stays hidden, and no inspection mechanic is designed. |
| History | Destruction ends the manifestation, not the season's history. |
| Eligibility | Full Deployment and Readiness still gate embodiment; viability governs consequences (AMO-D133). |
| Productivity | No formula introduced; AMO-Q116 untouched. |

**Result:** the individual rides inside its own fighter. Ordinary combat never reaches the core, and ordinary resolution ends the fight before the body is lost. Past that point, destruction exposes exactly the core the season has actually produced — where it fell, unable to move, sometimes worth rescuing, sometimes already gone, and sometimes leaving nothing behind but the Anchor.
