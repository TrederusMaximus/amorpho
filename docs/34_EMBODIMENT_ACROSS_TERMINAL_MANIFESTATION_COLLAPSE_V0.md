# 34 — Embodiment Across Terminal Manifestation Collapse, v0

**Status:** conceptual domain/boundary trace using owner-supplied design direction. It follows one inhabited mature Leaf across [Collapse Commitment](33_MATURE_LEAF_FUNCTIONAL_COLLAPSE_BOUNDARY_V0.md) and the end of its manifestation. It defines no duration, rate, threshold, readiness value, combat or KO rule, defeat condition, interface, animation, camera, network behaviour, species fact or implementation. It deliberately leaves the mapping from astral-state damage to biological persistence undecided (§10).

> **Collapse Commitment fixes fate; embodiment freezes progression. A manifestation that no longer exists cannot be inhabited.**

## 1. Purpose

[33](33_MATURE_LEAF_FUNCTIONAL_COLLAPSE_BOUNDARY_V0.md) established that a doomed Leaf may remain temporarily inhabitable, and that Terminal Leaf Collapse ends the manifestation (AMO-D115, AMO-D116). It did not say what happens to a Warden who is **already inside**. This trace answers that: when embodiment may continue, what embodiment does and does not change, where biology resumes, and where the Warden goes when the body stops existing.

The ontology it rests on is unchanged. The Warden's consciousness inhabits a **temporary manifestation**; the **Tuber** is the persistent biological individual; the **human body** remains in the world throughout (AMO-D028, AMO-D029, AMO-D030, AMO-D058, [09](09_EMBODIMENT_AND_ASTRAL_TRANSFER.md)).

## 2. Collapse Commitment versus Terminal Collapse

| Boundary | What it does | Effect on embodiment |
|---|---|---|
| **Collapse Commitment** | fixes the Leaf's biological fate: loss is irreversible and no recovery path remains (AMO-D115) | **none by itself** — inhabitation may continue (AMO-D116) |
| **Terminal Leaf Collapse** | the manifestation ends as a live form | **ends embodiment in that manifestation** — there is no body left to inhabit |

Between them lies the **terminal manifestation period**, in which the Leaf still physically exists, is biologically doomed, and — if the ordinary astral gates are satisfied — may still be a usable body. Commitment is a biological verdict, not a magical disconnect.

## 3. Inhabitability during the terminal period

A committed terminal Leaf remains subject to the same three gates as any other manifestation: a physical **Astral Anchor**, life-cycle accessibility, and biological inhabitability, with **Astral Readiness** separate again (AMO-D063, AMO-D086, [09](09_EMBODIMENT_AND_ASTRAL_TRANSFER.md) §7). Nothing about commitment satisfies those gates, and nothing about commitment automatically closes them. **Doomed is not the same as unusable.**

So a Warden may continue an embodiment that began before commitment, or enter afterwards while the manifestation still exists and the gates permit it. Where exactly inhabitability ends during terminal progression is not decided here and remains AMO-Q042, AMO-Q112 and AMO-Q115. No capability, combat property or duration is assigned to a terminal body.

**Terminal appearance is not a skin.** Visible terminal discoloration stands for a biological state — commitment reached, recovery unavailable, loss pending — and future systems may read that state rather than merely draw it (AMO-D115). Whether it carries situational gameplay value is an extension point only, with nothing designed or promised here (AMO-Q109).

## 4. Biological pause and persistent fate

Two established rules meet here and must both survive intact:

- while the individual is inhabited, its **normal biological simulation is suspended** — no growth, development, maturity gain, reproduction, life-cycle progression, Programmed Tuber Draw or biological recovery (AMO-D084, L49);
- **astral embodiment has no intrinsic maximum duration**, and no timer may be introduced to create one (AMO-D085, [20](20_BIOLOGY_MAGIC_BOUNDARY_AND_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md) §5).

Applied to a committed terminal Leaf, they produce a deliberate result:

```text
TERMINAL LEAF, INHABITED      → terminal progression suspended; state holds where it is
WARDEN EXITS / ROOTED LIFE    → terminal progression resumes from the same committed state
WARDEN RE-ENTERS              → progression pauses again, at the new state
```

**Ordinary biological terminal collapse therefore cannot complete underneath a continuously embodied Warden**, because completing it requires biological progression to be running. A Warden may in principle remain embodied in a doomed manifestation indefinitely and thereby postpone its end indefinitely.

**This is permitted by design, not an exploit to patch.** It follows the same reasoning already accepted for Bloom, where prolonged embodiment pauses the Programmed Tuber Draw and *that is allowed*, with no artificial timer added to prevent it ([20](20_BIOLOGY_MAGIC_BOUNDARY_AND_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md) §5, §7). This pass therefore introduces **no** maximum embodiment duration, terminal countdown, forced expulsion, magical strain, hidden deterioration or automatic readiness decay. (Whether a very long embodiment is bounded in practice depends on the unresolved **Astral Readiness** depletion model, which is an existing open question in its own right and must not be turned into an anti-exploit device — AMO-Q112.)

What embodiment does **not** do is change the state it paused:

> **Active embodiment may suspend biological progression; it does not undo biological state already reached.**

A terminal Leaf remains a terminal Leaf. There is no reset, no healed interval, no grace state produced by exiting, and no renewal produced by entering. Exit-and-re-enter is not a biological reset: each non-embodied interval lets the committed trajectory advance, and re-entry merely pauses it at wherever it has got to (AMO-D072, AMO-D086, L50). **Magic uses the current biological body; it does not resurrect a doomed one.**

The price of indefinite preservation is not decay. It is everything that is being deferred:

| Deferred while the Warden stays embodied | |
|---|---|
| **The terminal Leaf** | cannot complete its biological end |
| **The persistent Tuber** | cannot progress into consolidation, retreat, Dormancy or a later cycle |
| **Replacement routing** | cannot run, because it follows the manifestation's biological end ([33](33_MATURE_LEAF_FUNCTIONAL_COLLAPSE_BOUNDARY_V0.md) §9) |
| **The one direct presence** | is committed here and nowhere else (§11) |
| **The Human, other Amorphos, the World** | continue without the Warden ([20](20_BIOLOGY_MAGIC_BOUNDARY_AND_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md) §5, AMO-D009) |

*Theoretically possible does not have to mean strategically desirable.* A Warden may knowingly value a current playable body over progressing that individual's biology, and that is a legitimate choice, not a mistake to be punished by an invented cost.

## 5. Rooting a terminal Leaf

Rooting is the ordinary way an embodiment ends: the individual returns to biological plant state (AMO-D032, [09](09_EMBODIMENT_AND_ASTRAL_TRANSFER.md) §8). For a committed terminal Leaf that means:

- ordinary biology resumes, and with it the terminal trajectory, from exactly where it paused;
- **Collapse Commitment remains** — favorable Environmental Fit does not restore the doomed Leaf (AMO-D115, AMO-D094);
- Environmental Fit nonetheless acts on the **individual**, so good conditions may genuinely benefit the persistent Tuber's condition and future prospects ([12](12_ENVIRONMENT_AND_FIT_MODEL_V0.md), AMO-D033, AMO-D054);
- rooting is not a decline timer and never was (AMO-D034, L27).

So **rooting resumes biology; it does not rescue the Leaf.**

## 6. Terminal mobility: rescuing the individual, not the manifestation

A doomed manifestation still supplies locomotion, reach and access. A Warden may therefore use a terminal Leaf's remaining life to move the **persistent individual** toward a better climate, a greenhouse, suitable substrate, home or any other site the game later permits, and root it there before the manifestation ends.

The Leaf is not saved. The individual's future is repositioned, and whatever the Tuber later does — Replacement, consolidation, Dormancy, a later cycle — it does from that better place. This is an emergent consequence of rules already accepted, in the same spirit as strategic establishment ([09](09_EMBODIMENT_AND_ASTRAL_TRANSFER.md) §9). No mission, objective, reward, site rule or travel mechanic is designed here (AMO-Q043, AMO-Q046).

It also means the *timing* of the biological end is partly the Warden's to choose. The player cannot change **what** will happen to that Leaf; they can influence **when** biological progression is allowed to continue.

## 7. The manifestation-end embodiment boundary

At Terminal Leaf Collapse the manifestation ends, and with it the embodiment. This is ontology, not a player choice, an interface state or a combat abstraction:

> No manifestation, no manifestation inhabitation.

Explicitly rejected: a dead Leaf shell that the Warden keeps moving or fighting in; a playable corpse; a manifestation that remains inhabitable after it has ceased to exist; re-entry into a Leaf that is gone. Physical remains, if any are represented at all, are scenery rather than a body or a biological actor (AMO-D115).

The rule is **generic to temporary manifestations**. Nothing in it is Leaf-specific, so a future Bloom terminal model inherits it without new argument; no Bloom-specific behaviour is designed here (AMO-Q088).

## 8. Where the Warden goes

The consciousness cannot stay in a body that no longer exists, and this pass creates no new place for it to be. Under the existing model the human body has never left the world and remains the persistent home of the Warden's consciousness (AMO-D028, AMO-D029, [09](09_EMBODIMENT_AND_ASTRAL_TRANSFER.md) §3, §19). The smallest necessary rule is therefore:

> When the inhabited manifestation ceases to exist, direct embodiment ends and the Warden's consciousness returns to the persistent human body under the existing astral-return model.

**No free-floating astral avatar is created.** The architecture has three kinds of Warden presence — the human body, an inhabited manifestation, and strategic presence with a persistent individual that has no playable body — and none of them is a roaming ghost with locomotion, exploration or combat (AMO-D089, AMO-D099). A manifestation ending does not add a fourth.

Ordinary astral exit happens through rooting; here there is nothing to root, so the exact circumstances of an involuntary return — and of any forced exit generally — remain **AMO-Q113**, with no presentation, penalty or recovery behaviour assigned here.

## 9. Astral Window handoff

After the manifestation ends, the persistent Tuber may remain alive, and the existing transitional architecture takes over: the individual may stay astrally **reachable** for a biological period — the **Astral Window** — before Deep Dormancy closes it, and Tuber-level routing proceeds from there (AMO-D060, AMO-D099, [26](26_PLANT_AUTONOMY_ASTRAL_LEVERAGE_AND_ROUTING_V0.md), [28](28_ASTRAL_WINDOW_END_TO_END_TRACE_V0.md)).

Two distinctions matter and must not be blurred:

- **Reachable is not playable.** Strategic presence with a Tuber supplies no body, no locomotion, no combat and no exploration; the Tuber never becomes an avatar (AMO-D089).
- **Expulsion grants nothing.** Having been embodied a moment earlier does not confer strategic presence, routing control or priority. Direct presence must be established under the ordinary rules, and it occupies the same single consciousness (AMO-D101). Whether a just-returned Warden can immediately establish it remains AMO-Q119, with AMO-Q085 and AMO-Q115 owning reachability and signal.

## 10. Acute biological collapse, and one boundary left open

The acute path established in [33](33_MATURE_LEAF_FUNCTIONAL_COLLAPSE_BOUNDARY_V0.md) remains valid for a **rooted, biologically running** Leaf: a catastrophic World event may end the manifestation at once, with no meaningful terminal warning period, leaving the persistent Tuber if it survived (AMO-D115). Nothing here weakens [23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md), which still owns how an occurrence becomes exposure and then impairment for a rooted Leaf.

What this pass explicitly does **not** decide is the inhabited case. The existing boundary is that biology is suspended during embodiment, so an ordinary event affecting the inhabited fighter does **not** silently write new biological damage into the paused plant, and any exceptional carry-through needs explicit future design (AMO-D084, L49, [23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md)). Beyond that, the mapping is undecided, and **both tempting defaults are prohibited**:

| Not permitted as a silent default | Why |
|---|---|
| fighter damage or destruction **equals** biological Leaf damage or destruction | it would make combat an ordinary biological cost through the back door and collapse the harm horizons (AMO-D074, AMO-D079) |
| astral-state damage **disappears** when embodiment ends | it would make the fighting layer consequence-free by assumption rather than by decision |

A future specification must decide whether, when and how an inhabited manifestation can carry consequences that survive de-embodiment, distinguishing damage to the animated fighter, damage to the biological Leaf, what persists after the Warden leaves, and any Tuber consequence. That belongs to AMO-Q026 with AMO-Q113 and AMO-Q109, and this trace stops at the handoff (§13, trace D).

## 11. One Consciousness still applies

A terminal embodiment occupies the Warden's single direct presence exactly as any other does (AMO-D028, AMO-D101). While it continues, the Warden is not acting as the human, not inhabiting another Amorpho, and not exercising strategic presence with a different individual — including one that may be approaching its own routing point unattended (AMO-D097, AMO-D098). Multiple clients do not multiply that presence (AMO-D103, L53). No parallel-control exception is created, and no multitasking system is designed.

That opportunity cost is the natural limiter on terminal preservation, and it needs no supplement.

## 12. The sequence in one picture

```text
MATURE LEAF — VIABLE
   ↓  deterioration outruns recovery
COLLAPSE COMMITMENT                 fate fixed; embodiment unaffected
   ↓
TERMINAL MANIFESTATION PERIOD       may remain inhabitable; progression pauses while inhabited
   ↓  biology running
TERMINAL LEAF COLLAPSE              manifestation ends
   ↓
WARDEN EMBODIMENT ENDS              consciousness returns to the human body
   ↓
PERSISTENT TUBER REMAINS
   ↓
ASTRAL WINDOW · TUBER-LEVEL ROUTING as applicable
```

None of this adds a life-cycle state. The terminal period stays a condition of the **Active Leaf** manifestation, and there is no collapsed-Leaf state or actor ([17](17_LIFE_CYCLE_STATE_MACHINE_V0.md), AMO-D115).

## 13. Worked traces

**A — preserved through continuous embodiment.** A rooted Leaf reaches Collapse Commitment and begins its terminal period. The gates still permit entry, and the Warden enters. Terminal progression suspends and the Leaf holds at its current terminal state for as long as the embodiment continues, however long that is. Nothing heals; nothing decays. The Warden eventually chooses to root, biology resumes from the same committed state, and Terminal Leaf Collapse follows in due course. The Tuber remains. **Canonical.**

**B — intermittent embodiment.** The same terminal Leaf is entered, exited and re-entered while it still exists. Each rooted interval advances the terminal trajectory; each embodiment pauses it at the new state. No interval recovers anything and no re-entry resets anything. Progression is deferred, never reversed.

**C — relocate, then allow the end.** A doomed Leaf is inhabited specifically to travel. The Warden moves the individual to a markedly better environment and roots it there. Terminal progression resumes and the Leaf is lost as committed. The persistent Tuber now lives at the better site, and its later condition, routing and prospects are evaluated there. **The Leaf was not saved; the individual was repositioned.**

**D — external or combat damage while inhabited.** The Warden is embodied when an external or combat event occurs. Biology remains suspended, so no biological verdict follows from this trace: whether anything from that event survives de-embodiment is the **undecided astral-damage boundary** of §10. **Stop at the handoff** (AMO-Q026, AMO-Q113). No biological outcome, defeat rule or destruction rule is asserted here.

**E — entry after commitment.** A Leaf is already in its terminal period and visibly so. The gates permit normal Leaf entry, and the Warden enters, uses the manifestation, and later roots. No biological recovery becomes available at any point. Terminal fate is unchanged. This proves that **terminal is not inaccessible**.

**F — too late.** Terminal Leaf Collapse has completed. The Warden attempts Leaf embodiment and cannot: no Leaf body exists. The persistent Tuber may be reachable under the Astral Window rules, which is strategic contact rather than a body, and any intervention must go through Tuber-level routing.

**G — through to Dormancy.** After terminal loss the Tuber remains, Replacement is not feasible or not selected, the individual consolidates and retreats, the Astral Window narrows and Deep Dormancy closes astral access entirely (AMO-D060, AMO-D099). The handoff is complete and no new mechanism was needed.

## 14. Open issues and acceptance

AMO-Q112 owns Astral Readiness representation, what depletes it and whether it regenerates while embodied — including where inhabitability may end during terminal progression; nothing here turns it into a forced-exit device. AMO-Q113 owns forced exit and the exact behaviour of an involuntary return when a manifestation ends. AMO-Q026 owns the astral-damage → biological-persistence mapping named in §10, with AMO-Q109 for playable consequences. AMO-Q042 owns inhabitability and re-entry rules. AMO-Q119 owns whether a just-returned Warden may immediately establish strategic presence; AMO-Q085 and AMO-Q115 own reachability and signal. AMO-Q120 may later record terminal-period use in life-cycle history. AMO-Q088 owns Bloom's own terminal content. No new question was required.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Doomed but usable | Commitment alone ends nothing; the gates decide (AMO-D116). |
| No recovery by embodiment | Inhabiting a doomed Leaf never makes it viable again (AMO-D115). |
| Pause, not reset | Progression suspends while inhabited and resumes from the same state (AMO-D084, AMO-D117). |
| Indefinite preservation | Permitted by design; no timer, decay or expulsion is introduced (AMO-D085, AMO-D117). |
| Exit and re-enter | Each rooted interval advances the trajectory; re-entry only pauses it again. |
| Root to save the individual | Good conditions may benefit the Tuber; they never restore the Leaf. |
| Manifestation end | Embodiment ends with the manifestation; consciousness returns to the human body (AMO-D118). |
| No shell, no ghost | No playable corpse and no free-floating astral avatar. |
| Tuber continuity | The Leaf's end is not the individual's death; routing proceeds from the Tuber. |
| No automatic Tuber presence | Expulsion grants no strategic presence, control or priority (AMO-Q119). |
| One Consciousness | Terminal use occupies the single direct presence like any other embodiment. |
| Astral damage mapping | Explicitly undecided; neither equality nor consequence-free is permitted (AMO-D119). |

**Result:** a doomed manifestation is still a body. Commitment fixes what will happen to it; embodiment decides when. The Warden may hold a terminal Leaf indefinitely at the price of everything being deferred, may spend its last mobility on the persistent individual's future, and must leave when the manifestation itself ends — because a body that no longer exists cannot be inhabited, and what remains alive is the Tuber.
