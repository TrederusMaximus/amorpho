# 31 — Damaged Emergence to Imperfect Deployment, v0

**Status:** conceptual development trace using owner-supplied domain direction. It applies to primary Leaf, Replacement Leaf and Bloom within shared [Manifestation Emergence](29_REPLACEMENT_EMERGENCE_TO_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md). It defines no species response, damage formula, duration, threshold, resource currency, combat consequence or implementation.

> **While Emerging, the manifestation is still Tuber-funded construction. Damage may alter construction without ending it. Full Deployment transfers what biology actually built; it does not heal it.**

## 1. Scope and ownership

The Tuber remains the persistent individual and funds construction throughout Emergence ([30](30_EMERGENCE_INVESTMENT_AND_FULL_DEPLOYMENT_ACCOUNTING_BOUNDARY_V0.md), AMO-D109–AMO-D111). A developing Leaf or Bloom is not yet a mature manifestation and cannot normally be inhabited, even if visible or nearly complete (AMO-D107). Damage before Full Deployment first changes the **construction outcome**. Lost construction is sunk Tuber investment; no fraction of mature manifestation ownership transfers early.

For an acute occurrence, the ownership chain remains **World event → local exposure → developing manifestation response → developmental outcome**. The World supplies the occurrence and exposure context. Biology determines whether construction continues, changes course or fails. The same nominal event can have different outcomes with different developmental position, local protection, Tuber condition, Environmental Fit and developing-structure state (AMO-D035–AMO-D037, AMO-Q020, AMO-Q086). No event directly announces “Emergence failed.”

## 2. Developmental response is not binary

Damage during Emergence does **not** automatically end Emergence. The developing manifestation may continue with enough biological construction to approach a near-normal result, continue with lasting limitations, or arrest before Full Deployment. These are conceptual outcomes, not rigid states or an exhaustive classification:

| Possible outcome | Boundary result |
|---|---|
| **Continued development, near-normal result** | Construction compensates sufficiently; Full Deployment occurs with little lasting limitation. |
| **Continued development, compromised result** | Construction cannot fully compensate; Full Deployment still occurs, and the mature manifestation begins altered or limited. |
| **Developmental arrest or failure** | Full Deployment never occurs; no mature manifestation is created, and spent Tuber construction remains spent. |

**Developmental compensation** is the working description for continued construction around or after damage. It is not mature Leaf recovery, an assured repair mechanism, or a species claim. It may change what gets built. An imperfect outcome could be reduced, asymmetric, structurally imperfect or functionally limited, as future biology permits; no form or response is guaranteed for every manifestation type.

Developmental position matters in two independent ways. Later damage generally exposes more **sunk Tuber investment** (AMO-D109). Earlier damage may leave more **remaining construction opportunity** to compensate, while damage near deployment may leave less. Neither direction is a formula or a verdict: late damage can still yield imperfect Full Deployment, and early damage can still cause complete failure. Damage severity, spent investment and final manifestation quality are separate assessments (AMO-D110).

## 3. Imperfect Full Deployment

**Full Deployment means enough biological construction is complete to enter the mature manifestation state.** It does not require pristine, symmetrical, maximal or undamaged structure. A reduced Replacement Leaf, or a damaged but viable Leaf or Bloom, can be fully deployed **for the manifestation actually built**. Exact completion criteria remain AMO-Q084.

At that hard boundary, accounting and damage ownership hand off even when development was imperfect. The mature manifestation starts with its actual structure, function and limitations; deployment does not repair, normalize or refund anything. The Tuber retains the construction expenditure and the individual's history. This is a state-ownership handoff within one organism, not a new individual or a partial transfer.

For Leaf, the resulting mature **Structural Integrity** and **Leaf Functional Capacity** begin from that inherited developmental result; their derivation remains open (AMO-Q086). The first mature period of such a Leaf — stabilization, functional recovery, equilibrium or further decline — is traced in [32](32_IMPERFECTLY_DEPLOYED_MATURE_LEAF_STABILIZATION_OR_DECLINE_V0.md). Subsequent damage and possible stabilization or same-phase recovery use [23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md) and [24](24_SAME_PHASE_LEAF_RECOVERY_V0.md). Mature recovery may improve function through surviving structure, but cannot simply rebuild missing mature architecture into a pristine original (AMO-D094). For Bloom, the same handoff creates a mature Bloom state under its own damage and role rules, without inventing Bloom physiology. Full Deployment is still only the **biological prerequisite** for normal inhabitation; Astral Readiness and other access gates remain separate (AMO-D107).

Developmental impairment describes what happened **before** Full Deployment and what it caused biology to build. Mature manifestation damage describes what happens **after** deployment to the built form. Similar appearances can have different histories. A new manifestation does not inherit the old manifestation's structural damage (AMO-D074); it can nonetheless begin mature life impaired by damage sustained during its **own** Emergence. Neither history is erased at deployment.

## 4. Failure and future routing

If construction arrests before Full Deployment, there is no mature Leaf or Bloom state and no normal inhabitable form. The Tuber remains the individual with its sunk expenditure, changed condition and lost opportunity. Biology reevaluates feasible routes, including retreat, consolidation and Dormancy where appropriate; another attempt requires new feasible funding and time, never a free reset (AMO-D108, AMO-Q084, AMO-Q101).

Late failure can be especially costly through **both** large sunk construction investment and reduced remaining opportunity, while leaving the Tuber alive and structurally intact. Failed investment is not automatically Pathological Tuber Impact or Developmental Maturity regression (AMO-D092, AMO-Q118). Depletion can still affect later capacity and susceptibility through Current Biological Condition; actual persistent injury requires its own downstream biological path. The same separation holds for a damaged attempt that deploys: later mature damage belongs first to that manifestation, not as an immediate equivalent Tuber debit (AMO-D111).

Primary Leaf, Replacement Leaf and Bloom share this outcome range and the same Full Deployment handoff. Replacement can be particularly consequential after prior Leaf loss and expenditure, without a special damage multiplier or separate emergence physics. Bloom's normal Programmed Tuber Draw remains normal biological spending, distinct from failed construction and pathology (AMO-D087). Autonomous or Warden-compelled authorship changes none of these costs or outcomes (L52).

## 5. Worked traces

**A — early damage, continued construction.** The Tuber begins a Leaf's Emergence Sheath. Local exposure damages the developing structure early, when relatively little construction has been funded. Biology continues and development compensates where possible. Full Deployment produces a viable, somewhat altered Leaf. The hard handoff occurs; mature Leaf rules now apply. Early timing permitted this outcome but did not guarantee it.

**B — mid-Emergence compromised deployment.** Significant Tuber investment precedes damage to a developing Leaf. The attempt survives, but construction cannot fully compensate. Full Deployment occurs with reduced initial Structural Integrity and Leaf Functional Capacity in qualitative terms. Mature recovery may now operate from that impaired starting state; deployment itself supplied no healing.

**C — late failure.** A manifestation nears Full Deployment after substantial Tuber investment. Exposure disrupts remaining construction, and biology cannot complete it. No mature Leaf or Bloom state is created. The Tuber retains a high sunk cost and routes from its depleted condition and remaining opportunity. Failure is not automatically persistent injury.

**D — late damage, imperfect success.** Near the same boundary, damage occurs but enough viable construction remains. The manifestation fully deploys badly compromised. Ownership still hands off: its initial mature limitations and any subsequent mature damage belong to manifestation state. The Tuber is not directly debited for later damage merely because deployment was imperfect. Thus **late damage does not imply late failure**.

**E — Bloom.** A developing Bloom is damaged. It may continue to an imperfect Full Bloom Deployment, where mature Bloom state begins, or fail before deployment as lost Tuber-funded construction. Visibility or opening before Full Deployment never makes it normally inhabitable. Bloom-specific response remains open.

**F — Replacement Leaf.** After primary Leaf loss, the Tuber funds Replacement and its Emergence is damaged. One feasible outcome is imperfect Full Deployment of a smaller or limited mature Leaf; another is failure before the handoff. Both preserve the prior Leaf loss and Replacement expenditure. Neither supplies a free restart or instant Warden embodiment.

## 6. Deferred rules and acceptance

AMO-Q084 owns exact Full Deployment and failed-attempt topology. AMO-Q086 owns developmental susceptibility, damage-to-construction response, compensation, repeated damage, sheath/path damage, manifestation differences and how initial mature structure and function are determined. AMO-Q101 owns continuation/failure criteria, Environmental Fit and current-condition effects, future eligibility and routing. AMO-Q103 owns mature Leaf recovery **after** handoff. AMO-Q118 owns any later Pathological Tuber Impact. No new question, damage variable or physiology mechanism is introduced.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Early and mid damage | Construction may compensate or continue imperfectly to Full Deployment. |
| Late damage | It may cause high-cost failure **or** compromised Full Deployment; timing alone decides neither. |
| Handoff | Deployment transfers the actual constructed result without healing; no partial ownership or early playability. |
| Recovery | Developmental compensation precedes deployment; mature Leaf recovery follows it. |
| Shared process | Primary Leaf, Replacement Leaf and Bloom use the same boundary, with distinct mature biology. |
| Failure | Sunk cost remains, Tuber-level routing resumes, and pathology is not automatic. |

**Result:** a damaged manifestation can continue, deploy imperfectly or fail. Full Deployment remains the hard boundary even when the outcome is imperfect.
