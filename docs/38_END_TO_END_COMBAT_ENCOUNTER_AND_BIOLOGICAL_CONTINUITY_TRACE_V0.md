# 38 — End-to-End Combat Encounter and Biological Continuity Trace, v0

**Status:** integration and consistency trace of accepted rules, not a new system. One abstract individual is followed from rooted life through embodiment, protection, real body damage, a risk decision, resolution, relocation, rooting and recovery, with contrast branches through Functional Collapse and manifestation destruction. It defines no combat mechanic, HP, damage figure, shield or armour value, duration, recovery rate, Tuber mass, productivity value, item, interface or implementation, and it introduces no physiology.

**Acceptance question:** do [35](35_COMBAT_PROTECTION_MANIFESTATION_BODY_DAMAGE_AND_BIOLOGICAL_PERSISTENCE_V0.md), [36](36_COMBAT_RESOLUTION_SURRENDER_ESCAPE_AND_WITHDRAWAL_V0.md) and [37](37_MANIFESTATION_DESTRUCTION_TUBER_CORE_VIABILITY_AND_PHYSICAL_DROP_V0.md) compose with the existing biological architecture without contradiction, and does the intended gameplay loop actually run end to end?

> The fight is fought with a living body. What the body loses, it keeps; what the Warden decides, the season inherits.

## 1. Purpose and method

Every rule used here is already accepted: combat body damage and persistence (AMO-D120–AMO-D123), resolution families (AMO-D124–AMO-D128), the travelling core and destruction outcomes (AMO-D129–AMO-D134), the embodiment pause and manifestation-end handoff (AMO-D117, AMO-D118), mature Leaf impairment and recovery ([23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md), [24](24_SAME_PHASE_LEAF_RECOVERY_V0.md)), Functional Collapse ([33](33_MATURE_LEAF_FUNCTIONAL_COLLAPSE_BOUNDARY_V0.md)), Tuber-funded manifestation ([25](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md)), the Astral Window (AMO-D099) and One Consciousness (AMO-D028). **Nothing here redesigns them.** Where the trace found an ambiguity, §16 says so explicitly rather than quietly patching it.

## 2. Starting biological state

**Abstract Individual A.** No species, no values. Its persistent Tuber exists; its Leaf has reached Full Deployment and is biologically viable; the ordinary access and Astral Readiness gates are satisfied; the Leaf has already spent **meaningful rooted active time** before this encounter; the core is viable, with its reserve and productive state kept qualitative; the manifestation body is in reasonably good condition. The Warden has not yet entered.

This is deliberately **not** the earliest-possible-embodiment case: the normal management loop is tested first, and the fragile-core case appears only as contrast (§13, AMO-D133).

```text
ROOTED LEAF — ordinary biology running
   → Leaf performs its active biological role
   → Tuber persists as the core, funded this manifestation and may be receiving return from it
   → manifestation carries its current Structural Integrity and Leaf Functional Capacity
```

No productivity is calculated; AMO-Q116 remains open. What matters for this trace is only that **this history exists and is never erased** by what follows.

## 3. Embodiment

The Warden establishes direct embodiment. It is the **same physical Leaf**: no copy, no reset, no insulated fighter (AMO-D030, AMO-D120, AMO-D134). The Tuber travels inside it as the central core, with its Astral Anchor (AMO-D129, AMO-D132). Existing condition — including any scars from rooted life — carries in unchanged. Ordinary biological progression suspends: no growth, development, maturity gain, reproduction, life-cycle progression or **recovery** (AMO-D084, L49). The manifestation becomes mobile and playable.

Meanwhile the human body remains physically elsewhere, A's ordinary biology is the only biology paused, and every other plant and World process continues (AMO-D009, AMO-D029, AMO-D085).

## 4. Protection holds: the first effect

A enters a conflict. Protection is abstract — a magical shield, worn protection, a defensive action — with no item, value or mechanic implied (AMO-D122).

```text
INCOMING EFFECT → defensive response → protection intercepts
   → combat-state and/or protection consequence
   → the manifestation body is NOT reached
   → no new biological damage
```

This is the boundary working as designed: the fight has consequences and the plant has none yet.

## 5. Protection fails: the second effect

Later, an effect passes or overcomes enough protection to reach the body.

```text
INCOMING EFFECT → defence insufficient → residual effect reaches the Leaf body
   → REAL biological Leaf damage, immediately
```

No carryover event, no conversion, no waiting for de-embodiment: this is the same physical Leaf, and the Warden now fights on through a damaged one (AMO-D120).

## 6. Body damage enters ordinary mature biology

The injury is described with the **existing** vocabulary — changed Structural Integrity, changed Leaf Functional Capacity, an altered recovery and stability outlook — and nothing combat-specific is created ([23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md), AMO-Q086). The Leaf remains biologically viable. **Functional Collapse has not occurred** (AMO-D114). Ordinary recovery cannot begin, because recovery is one of the paused processes (AMO-D123).

## 7. Combat state and biological state diverge

The divergence is the architecture's central claim, and the trace exercises it directly:

> **A is still entirely capable of continuing the encounter, and its Leaf has already taken persistent biological damage.**

Combat capability answers whether this Amorpho can keep participating; biological condition answers what state the Leaf is in. Neither reads the other, in either direction (AMO-D121). Equally, a combat-state problem could have arisen with little biological damage at all.

## 8. The risk decision

The Warden now faces the decision that distinguishes this game. Known, conceptually: protection has weakened, the Leaf has taken real damage, further hits may worsen it, the manifestation still functions, and the objective is unresolved. Unknown, deliberately: the exact state of the core, which appearance does not report (AMO-D134). **The decision is made under uncertainty, and no scan, readout or interface exists to remove it.**

There is no correct answer and no utility calculation (AMO-D125). The trace takes the canonical branch — **Conscious Withdrawal** — and the alternatives follow in §13 and §14.

## 9. Conscious Withdrawal

The Warden judges that further biological risk is not worth this objective and stops participating. Note what is **not** true at that moment: the Leaf has not collapsed, combat capability is not exhausted, the Warden is not helpless, and no incapacitation occurred (AMO-D124).

The conflict context may then go against A — the immediate objective is conceded and the opponent obtains that outcome — while **the manifestation survives, the Warden survives, the persistent Tuber persists**. Losing the encounter is not losing the body. What the conflict costs remains context-specific and undefined here.

**The encounter ends; the embodiment does not.** No automatic rooting, expulsion or teleportation: the Warden has left a combat interaction, not a body (AMO-D126, L59).

## 10. Relocation and rooting

Because the damaged Leaf is still a viable embodied manifestation, the Warden uses it. Withdrawal is therefore **spatial as well as decisional**: disengage, move away from danger, and choose where to stop. No travel, route or map mechanic is implied.

```text
encounter over → Warden moves A to a safer, more favourable place
   → Warden exits direct embodiment → Leaf and its core root there
   → consciousness returns under the existing return model (AMO-D032, AMO-D118)
   → ordinary biological progression resumes — from the damaged state, with nothing reset
```

Where A roots matters biologically, because local conditions again drive its rooted life through Environmental Fit ([12](12_ENVIRONMENT_AND_FIT_MODEL_V0.md), AMO-D033, L27).

## 11. Recovery, and the rest of the season

Mature recovery takes over with no combat-specific path ([24](24_SAME_PHASE_LEAF_RECOVERY_V0.md), AMO-Q103): under favourable or adequate Fit the damage stabilizes, scars, and useful Functional Capacity may improve through surviving structure — but missing mature architecture does not regrow into its pristine form (AMO-D094). **The combat scar persists**: visible while fighting, still there once rooted, and still there if the Warden re-enters later (AMO-D134). There is no healing station, instant restore, repair action or timed fighter reset, and none may be added (AMO-D123).

In this trace the manifestation survives, stabilizes, remains sufficiently functional, needs no Replacement, and continues its active phase — the canonical desired outcome (AMO-D114, [32](32_IMPERFECTLY_DEPLOYED_MATURE_LEAF_STABILIZATION_OR_DECLINE_V0.md)). That is **opportunity preserved, not success guaranteed**: Fit and actual condition still decide what the rest of the season yields, and an intelligent withdrawal buys no biological promise.

What the Warden preserved, without any of it being valued here: the current manifestation, its remaining function, the remaining active-season opportunity, the persistent Tuber's future options, and the possibility of embodying this individual again. If biology stabilizes sufficiently and the gates are satisfied, **the same Leaf may fight again** — the same manifestation, with its actual scars and history, on no defined timetable (AMO-Q042, AMO-Q112).

The trace **stops before seasonal accounting.** The Leaf's remaining active period may still contribute biologically to the Tuber, and combat damage does not by itself create persistent Tuber loss. How much anything contributes is AMO-Q116; whether a neutral outcome ever becomes actual loss is AMO-Q118.

## 12. The canonical normal encounter, in one line each

```text
ROOTED VIABLE LEAF, with real biological history
   ↓ embodiment — same body, core carried inside, biology suspended
FIRST EFFECT INTERCEPTED — combat consequence only
   ↓
SECOND EFFECT REACHES THE BODY — real Leaf damage, immediately
   ↓
LEAF STILL COMBAT-CAPABLE while biologically damaged
   ↓
WARDEN JUDGES FURTHER RISK TOO HIGH — under uncertainty
   ↓
CONSCIOUS WITHDRAWAL — no collapse, no exhaustion, no knockout
   ↓
ENCOUNTER CONCEDED WITHOUT LOSING THE MANIFESTATION
   ↓
WARDEN REMAINS EMBODIED → relocates → roots
   ↓
BIOLOGICAL PROGRESSION RESUMES from the damaged state
   ↓
SCARRED BUT VIABLE LEAF STABILIZES AND CONTINUES THE SEASON
```

**The repository supports this trace with no new rule.** Every step is an accepted decision, and the loop it closes is *cultivate → embody → fight → protect → take real damage → judge risk → withdraw → relocate → root → recover → continue the season*.

## 13. The other three resolutions, compactly

**Surrender.** The Warden judges the risk unacceptable and explicitly yields; the opponent's encounter outcome is accepted and hostile continuation normally ends within the encounter; the manifestation remains embodied and viable, and the conflict cost stays context-specific (AMO-D125).

**Escape.** The Warden declines to concede and attempts physical disengagement. Whether it succeeds is a future combat mechanic and may be contested; there is no menu button and no teleportation. Success leaves the Warden embodied, mobile and away — carrying the biological body, and therefore the individual, with it (AMO-D125, AMO-D129).

**Objective resolution.** The reason to fight disappears — the objective is achieved or becomes impossible — and the encounter ends with neither body having failed and neither side yielding (AMO-D124).

In all three, as in withdrawal, the encounter's end is not the embodiment's end (AMO-D126).

## 14. Contrast branch: continuing instead

Return to §8 and continue fighting.

**Accumulating damage.** Further effects reach the body. Impairment increases while the Amorpho may well remain combat-capable, and recovery stays suspended throughout, so nothing offsets it (AMO-D123). This is player agency, not a punishment.

**Functional Collapse.** Eventually the damage costs the Leaf its active-role viability: Functional Collapse, then **Collapse Commitment**, using the existing biological boundary with no combat-specific variant ([33](33_MATURE_LEAF_FUNCTIONAL_COLLAPSE_BOUNDARY_V0.md), AMO-D114, AMO-D115).

**Still embodied.** Commitment ends no embodiment. The terminal fate is fixed, ordinary terminal progression is paused while embodiment continues, the Warden may in principle continue indefinitely, and further explicit injury can still damage the body. No timer is introduced (AMO-D117, [34](34_EMBODIMENT_ACROSS_TERMINAL_MANIFESTATION_COLLAPSE_V0.md)).

**Terminal withdrawal.** If the Warden stops here, the encounter resolves, the manifestation still exists, relocation is possible, and rooting resumes the committed trajectory: this Leaf cannot be saved, and the persistent individual can be — placed somewhere better, with its Tuber outcome downstream (AMO-D115, AMO-D131). **Withdrawal still matters after the body is doomed.**

## 15. Contrast branch: fighting to manifestation destruction

The Warden refuses surrender, escape and withdrawal, and further explicit injury physically destroys the body ([37](37_MANIFESTATION_DESTRUCTION_TUBER_CORE_VIABILITY_AND_PHYSICAL_DROP_V0.md), AMO-D127).

```text
MANIFESTATION DESTROYED → embodiment ends (AMO-D118)
   → the actual persistent core is exposed where A fell (AMO-D130)
   ├── VIABLE CORE     → living, immobile, non-playable Tuber + its Anchor remain on site;
   │                     nothing is awarded to the winner; physical rescue is required
   └── NON-VIABLE CORE → the persistent biological individual is lost;
                         the Anchor may remain alone as an object (AMO-D131, AMO-D132)
```

**Stop before rescue implementation.** Whether a given core is viable depends on the history the season actually produced, not on appearance and not on a calendar (AMO-D134, AMO-Q104). Two visibly similar battered Amorphos may sit on a robust core, a marginal one, or none.

### The escalation ladder

| Resolution | The manifestation | The individual |
|---|---|---|
| **Conscious Withdrawal** | survives, damaged; may complete the season | intact, with options preserved |
| **Terminal Withdrawal** | already doomed; will be lost | may still be preserved and repositioned |
| **Manifestation Destruction** | gone | survives only if the core was still viable |

## 16. Integration findings

**The architecture composes.** No contradiction was found in the normal trace or either contrast branch, and no new decision or law was required. Four findings are worth recording.

**1 — Combat can reach the individual's death, and only that way.** Everything short of death remains recoverable while the individual lives (AMO-D075), and combat produces no permanent *injury*. The one terminal outcome it can reach is death itself, and only through manifestation destruction leaving a non-viable core (AMO-D131). AMO-Q026's earlier note that combat cannot produce permanent injury without a new decision therefore still holds, correctly qualified: AMO-D131 supplied the death path, not an injury exception, and AMO-Q104 owns the criterion.

**2 — A Tuber has two transport modes, with two different rules.** Carried inside an embodied Amorpho, the individual's ordinary biology is suspended, so travel does not accrue rooted environmental consequence (AMO-D084, AMO-D129); traversal tolerance governs the animated body, and equipment protecting it never protects the rooted plant (AMO-Q051, AMO-Q048). Carried by a rescuer after exposure or excavation, ordinary biology **runs** — a Tuber does not become timeless because someone is carrying it (AMO-D093, AMO-Q117). Both statements are correct and they do not collide, because only the first mode is embodiment.

**3 — "Rounds" is pacing, not a win condition.** Fighting-game rounds remain a combat pillar (AMO-D008), and the resolution families are what end an encounter (AMO-D124). Doc 04 already states this next to the pillars, so no edit was needed; a future combat design may add an incapacitation concept without it becoming the foundational rule.

**4 — Withdrawal is spatial.** Because the core travels with the body, stopping the fight and choosing where to root are one continuous act, and the retreat site immediately matters biologically. That is what makes preserving a damaged body worth something beyond sentiment.

**Result:** one living individual can be cultivated, inhabited, fought with, damaged, judged, withdrawn, relocated, rooted and healed, and can carry on its season with its scars — or can be pushed past every exit until the body is gone and only the core decides whether it survives. Both run on the rules already written.
