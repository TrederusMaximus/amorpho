# 24 — Same-Phase Leaf Recovery Trace, v0

**Status:** conceptual integration trace using owner-supplied biological design input. It begins with the post-event state of a **fully deployed, rooted Active Leaf** from [23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md). It follows what can happen during the remaining active period and stops at an updated Leaf manifestation state. No species-specific fact, recovery rate, threshold, duration, formula, resource-cost magnitude or persistent Tuber outcome is specified.

> Recovery preserves the current manifestation where possible. Replacement creates a new manifestation when preservation is not enough. Neither rewinds biological time.

## Starting state and ownership

Abstract Individual A has one mature Leaf with impaired **Structural Integrity** and **Leaf Functional Capacity**. Its immediate ability to continue and its same-Leaf recovery prospect were assessed after the event, not guaranteed ([23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md), AMO-Q086). The Tuber remains the same persistent individual (AMO-D058, AMO-D087).

The **World** owns subsequent local conditions. **Environmental Fit** evaluates those conditions against A's biological response; it does not heal the Leaf by decree (AMO-D035–AMO-D037). The **Amorpho** owns current condition, the Leaf's structure and function, and its response over time. The recovery trajectory belongs to that biological evolution, not to a new World, Fit or Evolutionator subsystem. Reserves and stress may inform the response, but this trace neither updates nor accounts for them ([condition v0](14_CURRENT_BIOLOGICAL_CONDITION_V0.md), AMO-Q103).

## What can recover in the existing mature Leaf

Once a Leaf's emergence and expansion are complete, its basic architecture for this manifestation is established. Missing mature structure does **not** simply grow back into the original pristine Leaf during this phase (AMO-D094). Same-Leaf recovery can instead mean **stabilization** of damage, sealing or scarring, preservation of viable structure, improved mechanical stability where possible, and improved useful function through surviving structure. These are conceptual possibilities, not claims about a species-specific mechanism. A stabilized Leaf may remain **stable but scarred** and still do useful work. Its structural history remains visible.

**Structural Integrity** describes the existing Leaf's physical state. **Leaf Functional Capacity** describes useful biological work it can still potentially perform. They can change differently: function may improve substantially while structural loss remains; a relatively intact-looking Leaf may remain functionally poor (AMO-D091, L51). Compensation by remaining viable structure is functional recovery, not regrowth of missing architecture. **Stabilization** means deterioration stops; it does not promise regained function. Repair of an existing area, if possible, does not imply restoration of lost mature architecture. No universal recovery percentage or separate scarring meter is introduced.

The initial **same-Leaf recovery prospect** is only a possibility assessed from the post-event state. **Actual recovery** is the later trajectory under Fit, current condition and remaining phase opportunity. Similar initial impairment may improve, stabilize without improvement, or worsen. Continued poor conditions or another disturbance can interrupt or reverse improvement. This is a changing biological state, not an instant `recovered` flag or a wait-to-reset timer. Rates, extent and mechanism remain AMO-Q103.

## Time cannot be repaired

Functional improvement can change what the Leaf is capable of **from then on**. It cannot restore active biological time already elapsed. A Leaf recovering late may be capable again while little **Remaining Productive Opportunity** is left (AMO-D091). Immediate post-event continuation, a later recovery trajectory, and eventual usefulness are different observations: even a temporarily nonfunctional Leaf might become useful again if the life cycle allows it to remain active. Whether the phase continues or retreats is a life-cycle question (AMO-Q087, AMO-Q101); what changing capacity contributes to productivity is AMO-Q116. Neither is answered here.

## Trace A — stable but scarred, with meaningful functional recovery

A's mature Leaf begins visibly damaged and functionally impaired after Event A. Substantial active opportunity remains. Subsequent local conditions become favourable, Fit offers recovery opportunity, and A's current condition can support it. The injured structure stabilizes; a scarred or missing part does **not** re-form into pristine architecture. Surviving structure and function compensate, so Leaf Functional Capacity improves substantially while Structural Integrity remains visibly imperfect. The **same Leaf** continues meaningful biological work. **Stop:** this says what it can now do, not what the Tuber ultimately gains or loses.

## Trace B — stabilization without restored capability

Begin with a similarly impaired Leaf. Subsequent Fit is merely adequate. Further structural decline stops, but useful function does not improve meaningfully. The Leaf remains present and impaired. **Stable** is not the same as **functionally recovered**; no retreat or persistent outcome follows automatically from this observation.

## Trace C — replacement emergence is a different path

In a separate possible branch, the mature Leaf is too compromised to serve adequately. If the persistent individual is capable and the lifecycle permits it, the **Tuber may initiate replacement emergence** during the disrupted active period. A new Leaf manifestation develops; the old Leaf declines or is abandoned, and the new one assumes the active role. The old Leaf did not regenerate. Both manifestations belong to **A**, not to two plants. Whether and how their presence overlaps is unresolved (AMO-Q084). The eligibility and route selection are traced in [25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md).

The replacement may begin structurally intact yet smaller or otherwise reduced. Producing it uses biological resources and time and leaves less opportunity ahead; no cost or size rule is set. A's condition, reserves, Developmental Maturity, prior expenditure, damage history and any persistent harm remain A's history (AMO-D074, AMO-D080). A new structure is **not** a new individual or a reset. Replacement can restore future capability, never elapsed productive opportunity. It is **possible, not automatic**; ordinary next-cycle emergence after dormancy is a distinct circumstance. This trace stops once the replacement has an active Leaf state. The next specification reuses **Emergence** within the same active period; trigger, capacity requirement and timing remain AMO-Q101, and old/new Leaf overlap remains AMO-Q084.

## Trace D — recovery and replacement both fail

In another branch, poor subsequent Fit and unfavourable condition prevent useful stabilization; Functional Capacity remains poor or declines. The persistent individual also lacks a viable replacement opportunity in this circumstance. Active-phase failure becomes possible. **Stop:** whether that causes premature Senescence is AMO-Q087/AMO-Q101; neither retreat nor persistent Tuber consequence is decided here.

## Biological and magical boundaries

Astral exit and re-entry do not repair either Leaf (AMO-D072). While embodied, normal biological recovery and phase progression pause (AMO-D084); magical healing and Astral Readiness do not restore Leaf structure or function (AMO-D086). Movement or protection during embodiment might alter later rooted exposure, but does not directly reset biological damage. Replacement emergence creates no automatic answer about its Astral Signal, Readiness, entry window or transfer from the failing Leaf. Existing access rules remain authoritative; their application to this pathway stays with AMO-Q084, AMO-Q085, AMO-Q112 and AMO-Q115. Any playable capability of a scarred or reduced replacement Leaf remains AMO-Q109.

## Handoff and unfinished rules

The chain is now **World event → exposure (AMO-Q020) → event-to-Leaf response (AMO-Q086) → post-event Leaf state ([23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md)) → same-Leaf recovery trajectory (AMO-Q103)**. A Leaf that can continue remains on that path; **functional collapse hands off to [replacement routing](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md)** (AMO-Q084/AMO-Q101). The output here is the current active Leaf's updated Structural Integrity and Functional Capacity, together with whether it is stabilizing, improving or declining. Those are qualitative descriptions, not required stored fields.

Only **after** this boundary does AMO-Q116 integrate Functional Capacity over remaining opportunity into active-phase productivity. AMO-Q118 owns the later crossing into actual persistent loss. Leaf recovery is not persistent Tuber recovery; a replacement is not a resource refund; no result in these traces decides maturity, reserves, retreat, combat or Pathological Tuber Impact. Species scope and exceptions require future approved reality input, not research in this document (AMO-D024).
