# 33 — Mature Leaf Functional Collapse Boundary, v0

**Status:** conceptual boundary specification using owner-supplied design direction. It closes the seam between a mature Leaf that still serves and one that can no longer serve, and follows the terminal path from that seam to the end of the manifestation. It defines no threshold, percentage, rate, duration, confirmation window, productivity model, route selection, interface, combat mapping, species fact or implementation.

> **Imperfect is not collapsed. Scarred is not collapsed. Declining is not collapsed. Low productivity is not collapsed. Routing follows loss of function, not loss of form.**

## 1. Purpose

[23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md), [24](24_SAME_PHASE_LEAF_RECOVERY_V0.md) and [32](32_IMPERFECTLY_DEPLOYED_MATURE_LEAF_STABILIZATION_OR_DECLINE_V0.md) describe a mature Leaf that may be damaged, imperfectly deployed, scarred, stabilizing, recovering, stable at reduced capability or declining. [25](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md), [26](26_PLANT_AUTONOMY_ASTRAL_LEVERAGE_AND_ROUTING_V0.md), [28](28_ASTRAL_WINDOW_END_TO_END_TRACE_V0.md) and [17](17_LIFE_CYCLE_STATE_MACHINE_V0.md) all begin **after** a functional-collapse routing point they do not define. This document defines that boundary qualitatively, and the terminal path that follows it.

It answers one question: **when does a mature Leaf stop being merely imperfect, damaged or declining and become biologically incapable of continuing to serve as the active Leaf manifestation?**

## 2. Imperfect is not collapsed

A mature Leaf may be any of the following and remain a fully valid active manifestation:

- scarred, asymmetric or visibly damaged;
- structurally reduced;
- functionally reduced;
- imperfect from its own Emergence (AMO-D113);
- recovering, or stabilizing without recovering;
- stable at reduced capability for the remainder of the active phase;
- slowly declining while still serving;
- performing poorly.

**None of these is Functional Collapse.** Repeated microdamage, accumulated scarring and wear normally stay with the current Leaf while it remains viable (AMO-D096, L51). Collapse is a qualitatively different condition, not a further point along a scale of ugliness or weakness.

## 3. The canonical test: active-role viability

**A mature Leaf remains viable while it can continue to serve meaningfully as the active Leaf manifestation under its current biological trajectory. It has reached Functional Collapse when it can no longer meaningfully serve that role, even though physical tissue may remain.**

“Meaningfully” is deliberately not numeric. The shift being detected is `this Leaf still serves` → `this Leaf can no longer serve`.

The test is **baseline-independent**. It is evaluated from the Leaf's current biological capability, never as a fall below a hypothetical healthy original. A Leaf that deployed imperfectly may never have had an ideal mature baseline, and none is invented for it ([32](32_IMPERFECTLY_DEPLOYED_MATURE_LEAF_STABILIZATION_OR_DECLINE_V0.md), AMO-D113). The same test serves a near-normal Leaf that later declined and a Leaf that began mature life compromised.

**Collapse is role failure, not plant failure.** The role under evaluation is *serving as the active Leaf manifestation*. It is not death of the individual, destruction of all tissue, disappearance of the structure, or failure of the persistent Tuber.

## 4. What informs the boundary, and what does not decide it

| Input | Standing |
|---|---|
| **Leaf Functional Capacity** | Central, but not a lone threshold test. |
| **Structural Integrity** | Relevant; never the verdict. Appearance decides nothing (AMO-D091, L51). |
| **Stability and recovery direction** | Whether deterioration or stabilization currently dominates. |
| **Sustainability of remaining function** | Whether present function can be maintained at all, not only its level. |
| **Current biological condition** | The individual's capacity to support the manifestation ([14](14_CURRENT_BIOLOGICAL_CONDITION_V0.md)). |
| **Environmental Fit** | Influences the trajectory; it never sets a collapsed flag (AMO-D035–AMO-D037). |
| **Remaining Productive Opportunity** | Contextual for downstream routing value, not part of the definition (AMO-Q116). |

The evaluation is **contextual and derived**, not a stored verdict:

```text
WORLD                  → local conditions
ENVIRONMENTAL FIT      → derived biological boundary
MATURE LEAF STATE      → Structural Integrity · Functional Capacity · stability direction · condition
                             ↓
                   ACTIVE-ROLE VIABILITY EVALUATION
                             ↓
            CONTINUE                or         FUNCTIONAL COLLAPSE
```

No World-owned failure rule exists. The World supplies conditions; biology produces the outcome. **Appearance is never the verdict in either direction:** a moderately marked Leaf may have catastrophic functional loss, and a severely scarred one may remain highly functional (L51).

## 5. Direction matters: Collapse Commitment

Severe present impairment is not automatic Collapse, and a theoretical capacity to recover cannot postpone the boundary indefinitely. What matters is whether stabilization and recovery still preserve a serviceable Leaf:

```text
RECOVERY / STABILIZATION STILL SUFFICIENT
  → Leaf remains viable → continued active Leaf

DETERIORATION BECOMES IRREVERSIBLY DOMINANT
  → COLLAPSE COMMITMENT → terminal manifestation period → TERMINAL LEAF COLLAPSE
```

The upper path is the subject of [24](24_SAME_PHASE_LEAF_RECOVERY_V0.md) and [32](32_IMPERFECTLY_DEPLOYED_MATURE_LEAF_STABILIZATION_OR_DECLINE_V0.md); the lower path is this document's.

**Collapse Commitment** is that crossing: deterioration has outrun the Leaf's capacity to stabilize so that loss of this Leaf has become biologically inevitable. It is the same boundary as Functional Collapse, seen from the biological side rather than the service side, and it is the **routing point** already referenced by [17](17_LIFE_CYCLE_STATE_MACHINE_V0.md) §8, [25](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md), [26](26_PLANT_AUTONOMY_ASTRAL_LEVERAGE_AND_ROUTING_V0.md) and [28](28_ASTRAL_WINDOW_END_TO_END_TRACE_V0.md). No deterioration rate, recovery rate, comparison or threshold is defined; only the relationship between them.

What rehabilitation can actually offer before this boundary — stabilization, scarring, compensatory remodeling and substantial functional recovery up to a **Recovery Ceiling** — is specified in [40](40_ROOTED_MATURE_LEAF_RECOVERY_CEILING_AND_COMPENSATORY_REMODELING_V0.md) (AMO-D139–AMO-D141). Serious damage therefore does not imply inevitable Collapse; commitment arrives only when that rehabilitation can no longer preserve a viable active-role trajectory, and no threshold is implied.

**Commitment is irreversible for that Leaf's active role.** Later favorable conditions may change how the remaining terminal period unfolds, what it costs the individual and which routes stay open; they do not return the committed Leaf to normal active viability. Residual function after commitment is not renewed viability and does not reopen the boundary.

Distinguishing a **momentary functional dip** from confirmed Collapse remains necessary, and routing must not fire on a transient suppression that stabilization can still absorb. No timer, confirmation window or hysteresis rule is defined here (AMO-Q101, AMO-Q103).

## 6. The terminal manifestation period and its visible signal

Collapse Commitment is not the instant of physical disappearance. Between commitment and the end of the manifestation there is a **terminal manifestation period** in which the Leaf still physically exists, deterioration continues, the Leaf is destined to be lost, and no normal recovery path back to active viability exists.

When the path is gradual, that period is **visibly legible**: progressive discoloration — *terminal yellowing* — communicates that this manifestation is on its way out. Biology produces the warning; no numeric timer or interface is required, and none is designed. As with ordinary visible senescence, this is an owner-supplied **presentation direction** for the game, not a botanical claim about any species; approved input could later refine how a species shows it (AMO-D024).

Terminal yellowing and **programmed seasonal senescence** may look alike and must not be merged by cause:

| | Cause | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Programmed seasonal senescence** | the life cycle retiring the manifestation on its ordinary course ([17](17_LIFE_CYCLE_STATE_MACHINE_V0.md) §7–§8) | normal retirement |
| **Collapse-driven terminal senescence** | damage or deterioration making loss of this Leaf inevitable before or outside the ordinary end | failure of the active role |

The shared visual language is acceptable and useful: a yellowing Leaf says *this manifestation is going*. Which history produced it is a separate fact the game may later surface; no interface, cue or readout is designed here. Neither the terminal period's duration nor its stages are defined.

## 7. Acute collapse may bypass the warning

The gradual path is not mandatory. A sufficiently catastrophic mature-state event may destroy the Leaf's central biological role at once, so that commitment and terminal loss effectively coincide and no meaningful yellowing warning occurs. The acute path in [23](23_ACUTE_EVENT_TO_LEAF_IMPAIRMENT_V0.md) remains fully valid and unchanged: an acute event may produce recoverable impairment, lasting impairment, or immediate Functional Collapse, depending on the biological outcome. No event type, severity or threshold is defined.

Both pathways are canonical:

- **acute** — one event removes the active role; the terminal warning may be absent;
- **cumulative** — repeated impairment plus insufficient recovery and unfavourable conditions gradually exhaust serviceability, commit, and advertise themselves as terminal yellowing.

Microdamage is therefore never free and never a trigger by itself: many small injuries may eventually overwhelm compensation, but it is the **resulting functional state** that crosses the boundary, not the count of injuries (AMO-D096, L51).

Body damage taken while the Leaf was inhabited counts exactly like any other injury here. Accumulated combat damage — with rooted recovery suspended throughout a long embodiment — may therefore push a Leaf across this boundary, acutely or cumulatively, and it uses this criterion and the same Tuber-level routing with **no separate combat collapse** ([35](35_COMBAT_PROTECTION_MANIFESTATION_BODY_DAMAGE_AND_BIOLOGICAL_PERSISTENCE_V0.md), AMO-D120, AMO-D123).

## 8. Terminal Leaf Collapse ends the manifestation

At **Terminal Leaf Collapse** the Leaf manifestation ends as a live form. There is no persistent collapsed-Leaf state: no indefinitely hanging playable Leaf, no empty shell acting as a biological actor, no dead manifestation that remains inhabitable. Physical remains may be present in the World as scenery or debris; they are not the manifestation and not an actor.

The hard ontology after that point is unchanged from the architecture's foundation (AMO-D058, AMO-D095):

| | |
|---|---|
| **Alive persistent form** | the **Tuber**, with its identity, condition, reserves, Developmental Maturity and history |
| **No longer a live manifestation** | the collapsed Leaf |
| **Possible future form** | a **Replacement Leaf** built by the Tuber through ordinary Emergence, if biology permits ([29](29_REPLACEMENT_EMERGENCE_TO_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md)) |

*The Leaf is temporary. The Tuber is the persistent life form.*

## 9. Routing handoff

Functional Collapse **opens** routing; it never selects a route, and the Leaf never chooses. Evaluation is always Tuber-level: the persistent individual determines what is biologically feasible and, at most, a Warden with direct astral presence influences a still-available feasible route ([26](26_PLANT_AUTONOMY_ASTRAL_LEVERAGE_AND_ROUTING_V0.md), AMO-D097, AMO-D100, AMO-D101, L52).

```text
MATURE LEAF (viable) ──impairment / decline / recovery──▶ still viable → continue
        │
        └─ COLLAPSE COMMITMENT (functional-collapse routing point)
               │  terminal manifestation period, yellowing when gradual
               ▼
        TERMINAL LEAF COLLAPSE → no live Leaf manifestation
               ▼
        PERSISTENT TUBER
               ├─▶ Replacement Emergence, if feasible and selected
               └─▶ consolidation / retreat through Senescence toward Dormancy
```

Replacement eligibility and cost are specified in [25](25_TUBER_FUNDED_MANIFESTATION_AND_REPLACEMENT_ROUTING_V0.md) (AMO-D096); the retreat path is the existing Senescence route in [17](17_LIFE_CYCLE_STATE_MACHINE_V0.md) §8.

Routing may begin once commitment is established; it does not wait for the last tissue, and it does not depend on the dying Leaf as an actor. Equally, a committed terminal Leaf is not an indefinite active manifestation: the architecture converges on the Tuber. Whether a new Leaf's Emergence overlaps the old Leaf's remaining presence is still open (AMO-Q084), as are eligibility, autonomous policy and timing (AMO-Q101, AMO-Q119). When no viable playable manifestation remains, the existing **Astral Window** describes the reachable transitional Tuber (AMO-D099, [28](28_ASTRAL_WINDOW_END_TO_END_TRACE_V0.md)).

A fully deployed **Replacement Leaf** is a mature Leaf and uses this same boundary. Being smaller, reduced or produced late changes nothing about how its viability is judged; no special replacement-collapse rule exists.

## 10. What Functional Collapse is not

| Collapse is not | Because |
|---|---|
| **Tuber death** | the persistent individual remains alive, and may be healthy and fully capable (AMO-D058, AMO-D075). |
| **Pathological Tuber Impact** | persistent harm needs its own downstream biological path ([21](21_PATHOLOGICAL_TUBER_IMPACT_V0.md), AMO-D090–AMO-D092, AMO-Q118). |
| **Developmental Maturity regression** | maturity change is separately governed (AMO-D092, AMO-D093). |
| **Automatic Replacement** | Replacement is one possible route and only if biology permits it (AMO-D096, AMO-D098). |
| **Ordinary end-of-phase Senescence** | the visual language may overlap; the cause does not (§6). |
| **Low productivity** | a viable Leaf may perform poorly and still serve (§11). |
| **A cosmetic state** | appearance is never the verdict (L51). |

Collapse may cost lost productive opportunity, replacement expenditure or an early retreat, and those may have later persistent consequences. None of that is decided at this boundary.

## 11. Two boundaries, not one

| Boundary | Question | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional Collapse** (this document) | Can the current Leaf still serve as the active manifestation at all? | AMO-Q101, with AMO-Q103 |
| **Persistent outcome** | What did the active phase ultimately produce or cost the persistent Tuber? | AMO-Q116, AMO-Q118 |

They must not merge. A Leaf can be **viable but barely productive**, or **collapsed and no longer viable**; these are different statements. Collapse may end future useful contribution from that Leaf, but biological work already performed earlier in the phase remains part of the individual's history and is not erased by the manifestation's end. Active-phase productivity integration is untouched by this pass and remains AMO-Q116.

## 12. Astral boundary

Functional Collapse is a **biological** condition, and it means the same thing whether the Leaf is inhabited or rooted. A Warden may change exposure, position and environment; no Warden action redefines biological viability (AMO-D062, L52).

Two dimensions stay separate: **biological recoverability** and **astral inhabitability**. A Leaf may be biologically doomed, still physically manifested, and — if the existing readiness and access gates are met — still temporarily inhabitable. Committed terminal decline is therefore not an automatic magical disconnect, and the exact cutoff belongs to the biology ↔ magic boundary rather than to this document ([20](20_BIOLOGY_MAGIC_BOUNDARY_AND_ASTRAL_READINESS_V0.md), AMO-Q085, AMO-Q112, AMO-Q115).

What is fixed: **Terminal Leaf Collapse ends normal Leaf inhabitation**, because there is no longer a manifestation to enter; normal Leaf entry requires a fully deployed one (AMO-D107). Embodiment cannot change the committed fate: while embodied, biological progression and recovery both pause, so the terminal trajectory neither advances nor reverses, and it resumes from the same state once the individual is rooted again (AMO-D072, AMO-D084, AMO-D086, L49, L50). Exit and re-entry repair nothing. Continuous embodiment may nonetheless defer that progression for as long as it lasts, and an embodiment already under way when the manifestation ends is traced in [34](34_EMBODIMENT_ACROSS_TERMINAL_MANIFESTATION_COLLAPSE_V0.md) (AMO-D117, AMO-D118).

A terminal manifestation's distinctive visual state **may** later carry situational gameplay consequences before the Leaf is lost. That is recorded as an extension point only; no mission, concealment, camouflage or combat behaviour is designed or promised (AMO-Q109, AMO-Q026).

## 13. Worked traces

**A — scarred but viable.** An imperfectly deployed Leaf carries substantially reduced Structural Integrity with useful Functional Capacity. Fit is favorable, the Leaf stabilizes, and function remains sufficient to serve. **No Collapse**, no commitment, no yellowing; the Leaf remains the active manifestation. Appearance decided nothing.

**B — declining but still viable.** A compromised Leaf loses function gradually while stabilization still preserves a serviceable manifestation. It is getting worse and it still serves. **No Collapse yet**, and routing does not begin. Declining is not collapsed.

**C — gradual irreversible Collapse.** Deterioration continues to outrun recovery until loss of this Leaf becomes inevitable. **Collapse Commitment** occurs; terminal yellowing begins and communicates it; the Leaf remains physically present for a terminal period with no path back to active viability; **Terminal Leaf Collapse** ends the manifestation. The Tuber remains alive with its history, and Tuber-level routing determines what follows. **Stop** before route selection.

**D — acute Collapse without warning.** A viable Leaf meets a catastrophic mature-state event that destroys its central biological role at once. Part of the structure is still physically present; the role is gone. Commitment and terminal loss coincide, with no meaningful yellowing phase. The Tuber remains; routing becomes relevant. **Stop** before route selection.

**E — poor but not collapsed.** A Leaf has low Functional Capacity and compromised structure, and its biological output is disappointing, yet it remains stable and able to serve. It is **not** Functionally Collapsed. Productivity and viability are different questions (AMO-Q116).

**F — imperfect-start Collapse.** A Leaf begins mature life compromised from damaged Emergence, so no healthy mature baseline ever existed. Poor Fit drives further decline until current-state evaluation shows the active role can no longer be served. Collapse occurs **without reference to any hypothetical pristine Leaf**.

**G — terminal but inhabitable.** Commitment has occurred and the Leaf is terminally yellowing with no biological recovery available. The existing readiness and access gates still permit inhabitation, so the Warden may use this manifestation while it lasts; the biological state stays exactly as it is, and the pause during embodiment neither saves the Leaf nor advances its loss. Terminal Collapse later ends that opportunity. The embodiment side of this case is traced in [34](34_EMBODIMENT_ACROSS_TERMINAL_MANIFESTATION_COLLAPSE_V0.md). **Stop** before any mission, capability or combat design.

## 14. Deferred mechanics and acceptance

AMO-Q101 owns the exact viability criterion, confirmation of Collapse versus transient suppression, route eligibility and timing. AMO-Q103 owns recovery extent and whether stabilization can still avert commitment in a given state. AMO-Q084 owns terminal-Leaf persistence and old/new manifestation overlap. AMO-Q112, AMO-Q113 and AMO-Q115 own readiness, inhabitability and embodiment across the terminal period and the manifestation's end. AMO-Q109 owns any playable consequence of a terminal manifestation. AMO-Q116 owns productivity; AMO-Q118 owns persistent loss. No new question is required.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Ugly but viable | Severe scarring with stable useful function is **not** Collapse (trace A). |
| Declining but viable | Worsening while still serving is **not** Collapse (trace B). |
| Low productivity | Poor output with continued viability is **not** Collapse (trace E). |
| Acute Collapse | One event may remove the active role immediately, warning phase or not (trace D). |
| Cumulative Collapse | Accumulated impairment may exhaust serviceability and commit (trace C). |
| Imperfect start | Collapse is judged from current capability, with no invented baseline (trace F). |
| Irreversibility | After commitment, favorable conditions change the terminal period, not the outcome for that Leaf. |
| No lingering shell | Terminal Collapse ends the manifestation; the Tuber is the remaining living form. |
| Pathology and maturity | Neither follows automatically from Collapse. |
| Replacement | Opened as a route, never selected by this boundary or by the dying Leaf. |
| Astral independence | The biological definition is identical inhabited or rooted; doomed is not automatically unusable. |
| No embodiment escape | Biology pauses while embodied and resumes when rooted; deferring progression never reverses the committed fate ([34](34_EMBODIMENT_ACROSS_TERMINAL_MANIFESTATION_COLLAPSE_V0.md)). |

**Result:** a mature Leaf serves until it cannot. Below that boundary it may be scarred, impaired, declining or unproductive and still be the active manifestation. At the boundary, loss of that Leaf becomes committed — advertised by terminal yellowing when it is gradual, and possibly immediate when it is catastrophic — and the manifestation ends. What remains alive is the Tuber, and what happens next is a Tuber-level routing question this document does not answer.
