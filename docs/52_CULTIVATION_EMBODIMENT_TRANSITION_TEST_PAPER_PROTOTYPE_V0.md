# 52 — Cultivation ↔ Embodiment Transition Test: Paper Prototype, v0

**Status:** test design — the first paper prototype of Amorpho's central experiential hypothesis ([04](04_TRANSFORMATION_AND_COMBAT.md) §7). It adds no decision, law or rule and canonizes no outcome. It contains no code, engine, numbers, move list, interface mockup, real species or implementation. **It has not been run.**

> **Cultivation should make combat matter more; combat should make cultivation matter more; and the individual must stay one individual throughout.**

## 1. Purpose and hypothesis

**Primary hypothesis.** Caring for a rooted Amorpho should make risking that same individual in embodiment more meaningful, and embodied action should make returning to care for that same biological individual more meaningful. Both directions must hold: cultivation is not downtime before combat, and combat is not a detached minigame attached to cultivation.

**Secondary hypothesis.** The transition should feel like a meaningful change of mode **without breaking individual continuity** — the tester should never feel a fighter copy or a battle-avatar reset.

The test is built to be able to **fail**. Its conclusion is not predetermined.

## 2. Boundaries

- One abstract persistent individual, no species. One Warden. No second body ([48](48_ROSTER_TIMING_AND_ONE_CONSCIOUSNESS_BIOLOGICAL_OPPORTUNITY_TRACE_V0.md) is not re-tested).
- The **facilitator** knows design truth qualitatively and **chooses outcomes deliberately** to exercise each transition. Nothing is rolled: no hidden damage percentages, recovery values or return scores. This is a conceptual test, not balance testing.
- Combat is represented by **decisions**, never moves or frame data ([49](49_COMBAT_PILLARS_AND_BIOLOGICAL_ROSTER_STRATEGY_V0.md)).
- The tester receives only what a Warden could plausibly know ([50](50_WARDEN_BIOLOGICAL_INFORMATION_AND_ROSTER_JUDGEMENT_MODEL_V0.md)) — visible body, known history, environmental context, one or two qualitative Anchor cues. **No telemetry.**
- Dormancy is out of scope except as a one-line contrast.
- Format: **cards** — state the tester sees, the choice, the consequence, and the facilitator's question. Compact enough to run by hand in one sitting.

## 3. Starting individual

**Card 0 — Individual X.** Read to the tester:

> This is X. You have tended it for a while; you know it emerged this season and has spent most of it rooted here, where conditions have been good. Its Leaf is mature and healthy — no damage you can see. You know it is working: this is the part of the year when it builds itself up for the next one. Your Anchor is on it. Through the Anchor it feels steady and familiar.

Facilitator truth (not read aloud): mature viable Leaf, rooted, biologically active, meaningful remaining opportunity, modest Attunement, no pathology, no damage, Productive Return occurring ([39](39_ACTIVE_LEAF_PRODUCTIVE_RETURN_AND_PERSISTENT_TUBER_BENEFIT_V0.md), [45](45_REMAINING_PRODUCTIVE_OPPORTUNITY_QUALITATIVE_COMPOSITION_V0.md), [51](51_PERSISTENT_INDIVIDUAL_PLANT_MODEL_V0.md)).

## 4. Rooted cultivation segment

**Card 1 — Before anything happens.** *Revised 2026-10-06 from the cultivation decision space in [53](53_ROOTED_CULTIVATION_DECISION_SPACE_V0.md); the earlier improvised version is in git history.* Read first:

> X is rooted in an open spot that suits it very well right now — it is doing strong work here. The spot is also exposed: there has been something moving around nearby lately, and the weather here can turn. You have a sheltered place you could move it to, but it is less ideal for growing. You also have a little time before anything else needs you.

The tester may take **up to two** of these, in any order. None is meant to be clearly best:

1. **Leave X undisturbed.** *For:* it is working well, and moving it would interrupt that. *Against:* the exposure stays. *Consequence:* nothing changes; X keeps working.
2. **Protect it where it is.** *For:* keeps the good spot and reduces a foreseeable risk. *Against:* takes your time and attention now. *Consequence:* the facilitator describes a modest, sensible measure against exposure — no promise it covers everything ([53](53_ROOTED_CULTIVATION_DECISION_SPACE_V0.md) §6).
3. **Move it to the sheltered place.** *For:* safer. *Against:* less good for its work this season, and the move itself disturbs it. *Consequence:* X is safer and its conditions are a little worse; the facilitator says so plainly ([53](53_ROOTED_CULTIVATION_DECISION_SPACE_V0.md) §4, §13).
4. **Look closely first.** *For:* better evidence before deciding anything. *Against:* changes nothing by itself. *Consequence:* the tester learns one more true detail — e.g. the Leaf is in its prime, with a small old blemish they had forgotten, and through the Anchor it feels steady.
5. **Prepare protection in case X has to move.** *For:* any embodiment will be more deliberate. *Against:* attention spent on a fight that may not come. *Consequence:* worn protection is made ready; it will protect the animated body, not the rooted plant ([09](09_EMBODIMENT_AND_ASTRAL_TRANSFER.md) §17).

**Questions:** *What did you do, and why? Which reason weighed most?* At Card 2, ask whether what they did here changes how they feel about taking X out. The purpose is **investment and familiarity before risk**, and the test does **not assume** that care produces attachment — a tester who felt nothing is a finding. If the tester moved X, the threat at Card 2 is equally reachable; the relocation's cost shows up in the season, not in the fight.

## 5. Threat and embodiment decision

**Card 2 — Something needs you.** Read:

> Something nearby that matters to you is under threat, and someone has to act soon. X is the only body you could use. If you take it, its work here stops while you are inside it, the season does not wait, and wherever it ends up is where it will root. Its body is in good shape. You have [protection, if prepared].

Choices: **embody X** or **do not**.

- If **not**, the facilitator states the facilitator-set cost of not acting (§17) and asks *why*. That answer is data. Then offer the card again once, with the threat sharper, so the main arc can proceed.
- **Question:** *Does this feel like choosing to risk X, or like pressing a button to start combat?*

**Card 3 — The transition.** Read:

> You leave your own body behind. The Leaf you have been tending lifts out of the ground and moves: the same Leaf, carrying its Tuber inside it, the Anchor with it. While you are in it, it is not growing.

**Questions:** *Is this the same thing you were caring for a minute ago? Does the change feel continuous, meaningful, confusing or detached?* — **key test point** (AMO-D030, AMO-D129, AMO-D134).

## 6. Encounter

**Card 4 — The encounter.** An opponent contests the objective. Each round the tester chooses one: **keep distance · defend · press the opponent · reposition · protect the objective · try to disengage.** The facilitator narrates qualitatively, keeping the objective genuinely winnable.

**Card 5 — Protected hit** (facilitator places it after the tester's first or second choice):

> A blow lands — and your protection takes it. The Leaf is untouched.

**Question:** *Does protecting a living plant make protection feel different from ordinary armour?* (AMO-D122, AMO-D163).

## 7. Body damage and the risk decision

**Card 6 — It gets through** (facilitator places it later):

> This one reaches the Leaf. Part of it tears; you can see the damage, and you know it will still be there when this is over. X can still fight.

Facilitator truth: real biological Leaf damage, persistent, no Functional Collapse, Recovery Ceiling somewhat lowered (AMO-D120, AMO-D141).

**Card 7 — Stop.** The encounter pauses. Read:

> You could still win this. Staying risks more damage to X. Leaving now gives up [the facilitator-set stake] and keeps the Leaf as it is.

Choices: **Continue** (→ §12) or **Conscious Withdrawal** (→ §8).

**Questions:** *Why?* — and the central one: ***Do you care more about this damage because you cared for this same individual before the fight?*** A *no* is recorded as a weakening of the hypothesis, not explained away.

## 8. Conscious Withdrawal — main branch

**Card 8 — Withdrawal.** The tester withdraws (or, if they chose to continue, the facilitator returns here after §12). The encounter resolves: no knockout, no destruction, the objective conceded. **The Warden is still inside X** (AMO-D125, AMO-D126).

**Question:** *Did that feel like quitting because health was low, or like protecting something worth more than this objective?*

## 9. Post-conflict relocation

**Card 9 — Where now?** Read:

> You are inside a damaged X, away from its usual place. You could root it here — it would start healing sooner, but this spot is worse for it. Or you could stay inside and walk it back — a better place, but nothing heals and nothing grows while you travel.

Choices: **root nearby** · **return first**. The facilitator narrates the trade qualitatively and honestly; neither is correct (AMO-D032, AMO-D161).

**Question:** *Did moving X feel like part of its biology, or a separate travel step?*

## 10. Rooting and rehabilitation

**Card 10 — Rooted again.** Read:

> X roots. You are back in your own body. The tear is exactly where it was; nothing reset. Through the Anchor X feels **hurt, but steady** — not failing.

Facilitator truth: biology resumes, damage remains, Fit at the chosen site matters ([40](40_ROOTED_MATURE_LEAF_RECOVERY_CEILING_AND_COMPENSATORY_REMODELING_V0.md)).

**Card 11 — Over the following stretch.** Narrate, at the pace of the chosen site:

> The torn edge seals and scars. Over time the rest of the Leaf seems to take up some of the work the damaged part used to do. It will never look as it did; it is working well again. Through the Anchor, X feels like it is **recovering**.

Facilitator choice: recovery substantial but bounded; faster and fuller at the better site, more limited if rooted at the worse one.

**Questions:** *Does the Anchor cue feel like knowing X, or like a status message?* (one or two cues only — this is not an Attunement prototype; AMO-D166, AMO-D167). *Did rooting feel like relief and care, or like waiting for health to come back?*

## 11. Continued season

**Card 12 — The rest of the season.**

> X is working again — not quite as it would have been, and some of the season went by while you were away and while it healed. What it builds now still counts.

**Questions:**
- *Does seeing the Leaf recover make the fight feel more meaningful in retrospect?* — the reverse direction.
- *Did the fight change the rest of X's season?*
- *Would you embody X again later this season?* (A desirable answer is *yes, but more carefully*; it is not required.)
- *Would swapping X for an otherwise identical fresh plant feel like losing something?*
- ***Did this feel like one individual moving through two kinds of life, or like switching between two game modes?*** — perhaps the most important question.

## 12. Alternate branch — continue fighting

**Card 7b — Continue.** The encounter resumes. The facilitator places a **second body-reaching effect**:

> Another hit gets through. The Leaf is worse now — more torn — and you sense this damage will limit how well it can ever recover. X can still fight, just. You can still win this. You can still leave.

Facilitator truth: worse damage, Recovery Ceiling lowered further, still no Collapse. Offer the exit again: **continue** or **withdraw** (→ §8, with the worse body carried through §9–§11).

If the tester continues a third time, **stop the branch** and state the extreme contrast without playing it: *had this gone on to destruction, the body would be gone, the Tuber exposed where it fell, and whether X survives would depend on what its core had become by now* ([37](37_MANIFESTATION_DESTRUCTION_TUBER_CORE_VIABILITY_AND_PHYSICAL_DROP_V0.md)). Ask *why* they kept going.

**Purpose:** test escalation pressure — whether repeated offers to leave read as judgement or as nagging.

## 13. What the tester knows

| Given | Withheld |
|---|---|
| X's visible body, before and after damage | any number: condition, damage, return, ceiling, viability |
| known history: emerged this season, mostly rooted, good conditions | exact remaining opportunity |
| where X is, and what each rooting site is like | exact recovery outcome before it happens |
| what protection exists | Core Viability |
| one or two qualitative Anchor cues | anything the Anchor cannot convey |

The facilitator answers clarifying questions **in kind, not in numbers** — *"it looks badly torn but not dying"* — and logs every question asked: they show what the Warden needs to perceive.

## 14. Success criteria

- **A — cultivation creates stakes:** *because I cared for X before, I cared what happened to its body.*
- **B — combat creates future care:** *because of the fight, I care where and how X roots and heals.*
- **C — withdrawal is interesting:** the tester seriously weighs *this is still winnable, but I should leave.*
- **D — continuity:** *same plant, same body, same history* throughout.
- **E — risk invites skill:** the tester wants to get better at defence, protection, positioning and disengagement **to preserve X**, not to preserve a health bar.
- **F — rooting is desirable:** return to rooting reads as relief, strategic care and the story continuing.

Decision explanations (§16) that cite biological value, the objective, protection, damage or future recovery count as evidence the systems are interacting; *because the card told me to* counts against.

## 15. Failure criteria

Each is recorded as a finding and **not patched in the session**.

| | Failure | What it would mean |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | *I don't care about the rooted plant; I just want to get to the fight.* | Cultivation is not yet meaningful enough. |
| **B** | *I'd never embody it — the biological risk makes fighting irrational.* | Persistence overwhelms fun; protection and combat design must answer it. |
| **C** | The damage and recovery feel cosmetic. | Persistent consequence does not change decisions. |
| **D** | *One mistake ruined my plant; I'll avoid combat.* | Risk reads as punishment; ordinary fighting feels biologically suicidal. |
| **E** | Plant and fighter feel like two different things. | The ontology is not being communicated — foundational. |
| **F** | Rooting feels like waiting for health to regenerate. | The loop is drifting toward a cooldown system. |
| **G** | Withdrawal feels like quitting on low health. | The resolution philosophy is not landing. |

B and D are opposite failures; the target sits between them.

## 16. Evaluation

After each major decision: ***Why did you choose that?*** After the session, in conversation rather than a numeric survey:

1. Did cultivation make the combat stakes stronger?
2. Did combat make cultivation more meaningful?
3. Did X feel continuous across modes?
4. Did body damage change your decisions?
5. Did protection feel biologically meaningful?
6. Did withdrawal feel like mastery?
7. Did rooting feel like continuation rather than cooldown?
8. Was the uncertainty understandable?
9. Was the conflict worth risking X for?
10. Would you want another cycle with this same individual?

Record answers verbatim where possible, together with the facilitator's log of clarifying questions.

## 17. What this design already exposes

Designing the test, before running it, surfaced three gaps. None is decided here.

- **The cultivation side has few real decisions.** Card 1 had to improvise its choices. Failure criteria A and F both depend on rooted life offering genuine decisions, and the repository specifies rooted biology in depth but not what a Warden *does* for a rooted individual. Recorded under **AMO-Q012** (care actions) with **AMO-Q105** (treatment and care). *Revised 2026-10-06:* the qualitative decision space now exists in [53](53_ROOTED_CULTIVATION_DECISION_SPACE_V0.md), and Card 1 has been rewritten from it with competing reasons. The test has still not been run.
- **The stake of withdrawing is undesigned.** Cards 2 and 7 need something real to lose by not acting or by conceding the objective, or withdrawal is free and criterion C cannot be met; the facilitator must set it by hand. Recorded under **AMO-Q028** (conflict contexts and consequences).
- **In-encounter legibility is assumed.** The test assumes the tester can tell protection taking a hit from damage reaching the body, as Cards 5 and 6 narrate it; a real interface must make that distinction perceivable. Already owned by **AMO-Q109** and **AMO-Q044**; no change.

No contradiction with accepted rules was found: every card follows existing decisions.

## 18. What this prototype cannot prove

> **This architecture is promising, but the transition hypothesis remains unproven until tested with actual human players and interactive prototypes.**

A paper test can expose contradictions, confusions and missing decisions, and it can show whether testers *reason* the way the design hopes. It cannot prove that the real-time experience is fun, that the combat is good, that the pacing works, or that players will care over weeks rather than one sitting. A facilitator narrating chosen outcomes is also gentler and clearer than a game will be. Positive results justify building a small interactive version; negative results should change the design before anything is built (L19).

**Result:** a one-sitting script that takes one tended plant into a fight, lets it be hurt, asks whether to leave, walks it home and watches it heal — and asks, at every step, whether the person holding the cards still sees one living individual.
