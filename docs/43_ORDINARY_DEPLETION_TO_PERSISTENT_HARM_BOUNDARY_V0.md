# 43 — Ordinary Depletion to Persistent Harm Boundary, v0

**Status:** qualitative boundary pass using owner-supplied domain direction. It resolves the conceptual core of AMO-Q118 — where a poor biological outcome stops being depletion and becomes actual persistent harm. It defines no threshold, percentage, unit, reserve value, Tuber mass, formula, rate, recovery time, species vulnerability or diagnostic, and it designs **no disease, symptom, treatment or repair system**.

> **Depletion spends capacity; pathology damages persistence. A plant can be dangerously empty and still be entirely unharmed.**

## 1. Purpose and scope

[21](21_PATHOLOGICAL_TUBER_IMPACT_V0.md) fixed what **Pathological Tuber Impact** means and listed what does not qualify. What it never defined positively was the other side of the line — **ordinary depletion** — nor what changes ontologically when the line is crossed. Every later trace ([32](32_IMPERFECTLY_DEPLOYED_MATURE_LEAF_STABILIZATION_OR_DECLINE_V0.md), [37](37_MANIFESTATION_DESTRUCTION_TUBER_CORE_VIABILITY_AND_PHYSICAL_DROP_V0.md), [39](39_ACTIVE_LEAF_PRODUCTIVE_RETURN_AND_PERSISTENT_TUBER_BENEFIT_V0.md), [40](40_ROOTED_MATURE_LEAF_RECOVERY_CEILING_AND_COMPENSATORY_REMODELING_V0.md), [42](42_WORLD_TIME_SEASONAL_OPPORTUNITY_AND_EMBODIMENT_V0.md)) deferred to that line, so it is defined here.

**Deliberately narrow.** Persistent pathology is accepted as a real future biological layer — later it may become a substantial management domain with causes such as pathogens, parasites, environmental or physical injury and cumulative stress, and possibly Warden-side noticing, diagnosis, intervention and treatment. **None of that is designed here, and no specific cause, symptom, susceptibility or treatment is canonised.** This pass supplies only the boundary, which the architecture needs now. The detailed system is a lower-priority future layer beside the core loop, not inside it.

## 2. Depletion is not damage

> **Ordinary depletion** is a reduction in currently available persistent biological capacity caused by legitimate expenditure, insufficient replenishment or missed productive return, while the persistent structures, capabilities and condition themselves remain fundamentally recoverable through ordinary future biology.

> **Persistent harmful loss** exists when the Tuber has lost or degraded persistent biological structure, capability or condition in a way that is not merely the absence of replenishment and cannot be described as ordinary recoverable depletion alone.

The distinction is between *less available right now* and *something persistent has actually been harmed*. A Tuber may be less provisioned, less buffered, poorly prepared for its next manifestation and genuinely vulnerable if the current body is lost — all without Pathological Tuber Impact.

A useful test, deliberately not a timer:

| Ask | If yes |
|---|---|
| Could ordinary successful future biological life restore this, because the problem *is* depletion? | ordinary depletion |
| Has the individual lost something enduring that ordinary replenishment alone does not simply undo? | persistent harm |

This must not become *recovers within X = depletion*. There is no duration rule, and it must not collapse into "anything hard to recover is pathology" either: depletion is the **kind of state** normal successful biology replenishes, while pathology means something beyond capacity has been harmed.

## 3. What is depletion, not harm

Each of these is ordinary biological state change (AMO-D092, AMO-D138, AMO-D145):

- **legitimate expenditure** — funding a manifestation, funding a Replacement, Bloom's **Programmed Tuber Draw** (AMO-D087, AMO-D109, AMO-D096);
- **insufficient Productive Return** — a Leaf that functioned poorly and left little persistent benefit;
- **missed Productive Opportunity** — a favourable window spent embodied, or conditions that passed unused;
- **ending a weak season poorly replenished**;
- **stress**, however high, which recovers without persistent loss (AMO-D056).

> **Paying a legitimate biological cost does not mean the Tuber was injured, and what never happened is not an injury.**

**Severe depletion is still not pathology.** A Tuber may be dangerously depleted while remaining structurally and biologically intact, producing low Core Viability, weak future manifestation potential and heightened vulnerability with no persistent harm anywhere. **Severity and pathology are different axes**, and no amount of *low* converts into *damaged*.

Two consequences matter especially:

**Vulnerability is not injury.** Heavy legitimate spending may leave little buffering and poor tolerance for further adversity. That changes **susceptibility**, not classification (AMO-D092, §6).

**Death does not retroactively pathologize expenditure.** A healthy Tuber may spend heavily on a manifestation, have that manifestation destroyed early, and prove unable to survive on what remained — the individual is lost through the *combination* of state and catastrophic event, and the earlier expenditure remains ordinary expenditure (AMO-D131, [37](37_MANIFESTATION_DESTRUCTION_TUBER_CORE_VIABILITY_AND_PHYSICAL_DROP_V0.md)). History is not rewritten to blame the spending.

Missed opportunity can likewise **contribute causally** to a later harmful outcome — a Tuber left unreplenished may be overwhelmed by a stress it would otherwise have tolerated — without the missed opportunity itself ever being pathology. Causal contribution and classification are different things.

## 4. Crossing the boundary

Pathology has the two established pathways, and no third is created (AMO-D090, [21](21_PATHOLOGICAL_TUBER_IMPACT_V0.md) §2):

| | How |
|---|---|
| **Direct** | the Tuber itself is harmed, which needs no productive accounting to matter and may occur with high reserves intact |
| **Indirect** | manifestation and environmental history eventually produce actual persistent degradation beyond ordinary depletion |

The indirect route needs one more step than a bad season, and this is the conceptual handoff:

```text
MANIFESTATION FAILURE / POOR RETURN / STRESS / MISSED OPPORTUNITY
   ↓
ORDINARY DEPLETION · LOST OPPORTUNITY
   ↓  is the persistent individual itself still intact?
   ├── YES → non-pathological poor outcome, however disappointing
   └── NO  → actual persistent degradation → Pathological Tuber Impact
```

**Accumulation may cross it.** Repeated failed manifestations, repeated severe under-replenishment, prolonged adverse conditions, repeated costly recovery or Replacement demands and cumulative strain can end in genuine persistent loss. But **no one of them causes pathology automatically, and no event count crosses anything**: the boundary is crossed when persistent capability has actually degraded (AMO-D092).

**Crossing changes the ontology, not a value.** Before it, *the individual is depleted*; after it, *the individual has been harmed*. That is a different biological fact, not a worse reading on one meter — which is why no single Tuber health score may exist.

Poor Environmental Fit can act through several of these pathways at once — reducing return, preventing recovery, sustaining depletion, creating stress and eventually contributing to harm — so **poor Fit is never pathology by itself**; the outcome decides (AMO-D035–AMO-D037).

## 5. Persistent, not permanent

Persistent harmful loss means the consequence belongs to the **persistent Tuber and survives manifestation turnover**. It does not mean irreversible, untreatable or lethal (AMO-D075).

What cannot erase it:

- **a new manifestation** — old Leaf scars end with the old Leaf, while Tuber pathology stays with the individual (AMO-D074);
- **Replacement** — fresh architecture is not a Tuber reset (AMO-D096);
- **dormancy** — a cycle boundary is not a repair event (AMO-D060, [21](21_PATHOLOGICAL_TUBER_IMPACT_V0.md) §7);
- **elapsed time** alone (AMO-D145).

Only a genuine persistent-recovery process could, and **no such process is designed here**: repair, treatment, rates, reversibility and any scar-like persistent outcome are all deliberately left open (AMO-Q102, AMO-Q105).

**Replenishment and repair are separate concepts** and must stay so:

| | Restores |
|---|---|
| **Replenishment** | available biological capacity, through ordinary successful biology |
| **Persistent recovery / repair** | actual persistent harm to the Tuber |

A Tuber may need **both at once**, and neither collapses into "health". Ordinary depletion has a complete non-pathological path back — depleted Tuber, later successful rooted Leaf, Productive Return, capacity rebuilt — with no healing event required (AMO-D135). Pathology needs more than replenishment, and later Productive Return does not make the harm not have happened.

## 6. Adjacent dimensions that must stay separate

**Core Viability is not pathology.** A Tuber may be **non-viable without ever having been harmed**, simply too depleted to continue independently, and **pathological while still viable**, because persistent damage occurred yet enough function remains. Core Viability is derived from the individual's total actual state and does not reveal which pathway produced the vulnerability (AMO-D131, AMO-D151).

**Developmental Maturity is not condition.** Pathology does not automatically regress maturity; regression has its own requirement (AMO-D082, AMO-D092). A severely harmed or depleted individual may nonetheless be **unable to express** the maturity it genuinely has — a useful distinction, with no formula and no automatic coupling (AMO-Q106).

**Recovery Ceiling is manifestation-owned; pathology is Tuber-owned.** A Leaf's ceiling ends with that Leaf; persistent harm continues across manifestations (AMO-D141).

**Persistent condition stays multidimensional.** Available capacity, Core Viability, Developmental Maturity and pathological harm are qualitatively different facts, and no global Tuber health value, pathology score, damage meter or reserve percentage may represent them (AMO-D056, [21](21_PATHOLOGICAL_TUBER_IMPACT_V0.md) §5).

Pathology and Productive Return also **coexist freely in both directions**: an injured Tuber may still receive useful work, and a poorly performing Leaf may accompany a direct injury. Nothing nets them into one figure (AMO-D138).

## 7. Cause matters more than level

Two individuals may show the same apparent available capacity and not be in the same state at all:

| | Same low capacity because |
|---|---|
| **Tuber A** | it just funded a healthy manifestation — ordinarily depleted |
| **Tuber B** | persistent capability was harmed — pathologically compromised |

They are not equivalent, and **state amount does not determine state cause.** Pathology may never be inferred from how low current capacity looks, which is precisely what keeps a single scalar Tuber resource from being sufficient.

The model therefore needs to know enough **history** to distinguish expenditure, missed gain, depletion, direct injury and persistent degradation. That is a conceptual requirement only: **no schema, event log or record format is designed**, and future individual history may carry it however it likes (AMO-Q120).

Susceptibility is history-sensitive too: the same adverse event may be tolerable to a robust Tuber and produce persistent harm in a depleted one (AMO-D092, AMO-Q108). And pathology **need not announce itself** — persistent harm may exist before any dramatic external effect, no diagnostic is defined, and appearance remains no verdict for the Tuber any more than for a Leaf (AMO-D134, L51).

Species strategies may differ in expected depletion, dormancy behaviour, Programmed Draw and resilience, so pathology means *actual harmful persistent consequence relative to what that individual's biology can normally sustain and recover from* — never *departure from maximum condition*. No species facts, normal ranges or vulnerabilities are defined (AMO-D147, AMO-D024).

## 8. The outcome spectrum

Conceptual examples, not an enumeration or a set of states:

- **healthy and well provisioned** — strong persistent position;
- **intact but depleted** — low capacity, no pathology;
- **intact but poorly replenished** after a weak or missed season — still no pathology;
- **pathologically compromised but viable** — persistent harm exists and the individual continues;
- **pathologically compromised and severely depleted** — both problems at once;
- **non-viable** — persistence fails, with or without any prior pathology.

## 9. Worked contrasts

**A — expensive Emergence.** A Tuber funds a manifestation and is left deeply depleted. Nothing was harmed. **Ordinary depletion**, and future successful rooted work may rebuild it.

**B — Bloom Programmed Draw.** A Tuber strong enough to bloom does so, and substantial Programmed Draw leaves it heavily depleted while biologically intact. **No pathology** — and if this case were classified as harm, the Bloom architecture would break (AMO-D087, L63).

**C — the missed season.** A Warden remains embodied through the favourable window; little Productive Return occurs; the Tuber ends weak. **No pathology**, however poor the timing decision was (AMO-D145, L64).

**D — disappointing season.** The Leaf functioned badly under poor Fit and produced little benefit. The Tuber is depleted and unimproved. **No pathology** (AMO-D138).

**E — early destruction and loss.** After heavy legitimate construction spending, the manifestation is destroyed early and the remaining core cannot survive. The individual is lost. **The prior expenditure was still not pathological** (AMO-D131).

**F — direct injury.** A well-provisioned Tuber is physically harmed and loses persistent structure or capability. **Pathology exists even with high reserves** — proving pathology is not low capacity (AMO-D090).

**G — cumulative crossing.** A Tuber begins depleted; adverse episodes repeat; replenishment repeatedly fails; eventually persistent capability itself degrades. The state can no longer be described as depletion alone, so **Pathological Tuber Impact exists** — and when exactly is not defined.

**H — pathology with useful work.** A Tuber suffers direct persistent injury while its Leaf later performs real biological work. Both facts stand, and the persistent outcome reflects both without netting.

**I — turnover changes nothing.** An individual with existing Tuber pathology loses its Leaf, passes through dormancy and later emerges with fresh architecture. **The persistent harm is still there.**

## 10. Deferred model and acceptance

AMO-Q118 keeps everything quantitative: representation, exact persistent-harm criteria, cumulative integration, severity, susceptibility, species and individual variation, and the mapping from adverse history to outcome. AMO-Q108 keeps the crossing's mechanics; AMO-Q102 persistent-recovery dynamics; **AMO-Q105 is the single broad owner of any future persistent repair, care or pathology-management layer** — no per-cause, per-disease, per-symptom or per-treatment question may be created. AMO-Q117 owns direct susceptibility and transport, AMO-Q104 Core Viability, AMO-Q106 maturity representation, AMO-Q103 manifestation recovery, AMO-Q116 productivity, AMO-Q120 any historical record. No new question was required.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Expensive Emergence | Deep depletion with nothing harmed is not pathology (AMO-D148). |
| Programmed Draw | Heavy legitimate spending is never pathology by itself (AMO-D087). |
| Missed season | Poor replenishment after lost opportunity is not pathology (AMO-D145). |
| Weak return | A disappointing season is not injury (AMO-D138). |
| Non-viable early core | An individual may be lost without prior pathology (AMO-D151). |
| Direct injury | Pathology may exist with capacity intact (AMO-D090). |
| Cumulative harm | Crossing requires actual degradation of persistence, never an event count (AMO-D149). |
| Viable pathology | Persistent harm may coexist with continued viability. |
| Depleted but intact | Ordinary future biology can replenish it; no repair process is needed (AMO-D150). |
| New manifestation | Fresh architecture does not erase Tuber pathology. |
| Dormancy and time | Neither resets persistent harm. |
| Maturity | Pathology does not regress maturity, though it may limit expressing it (AMO-D082). |
| Cause versus level | Identical low capacity may be depletion or harm; history decides (AMO-D148). |
| No score | No Tuber health value, pathology meter or reserve percentage exists. |

**Result:** the repository can now say *this Tuber is merely depleted* or *this Tuber has suffered persistent harm*, and can keep the two apart through a whole season of expensive, disappointing, badly timed biology. It cannot yet say what harmed it, how to see it, or how to cure it — and deliberately does not need to.
